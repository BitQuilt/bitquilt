# QX-23: a fork inherits squares

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 10 November 2026 in the reference tracker and the Quilt client; [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [QX-22](QX-22.md) is the proposed pieces a fork may hold where an owner leaves them, and [QX-20](QX-20.md) is the record kind, whose `recorded` a dependent unlocks on as it does here on a square made elsewhere. A core client ignores all of it: a fork starts empty in the core, and a core client computes the same map of it that it did before.

**Abstract.** A fork disputes the frame and keeps the facts. A piece is named by the hash of its brief, its schema and its verifier ([CORE.md](../CORE.md) section 3), so a piece a fork keeps unchanged is the same question, and the square it has on the problem the fork came from already answers it. In the core a fork inherits nothing, and a forker who wants to pursue one more question must have every square made again. This extension lets the tracker's layer read, on a fork, the square a piece has on the problem it forks: the piece says `inherited` and names the problem where the square stands, and the pieces that wait on it are open for work with its returns in their briefs. An inherited square is a reading of the record and moves nothing: no event is added, nothing is accepted or struck, no standing is credited and no coin is earned. The fork's own work on any piece is taken as before, and where it makes a square the fork shows its own.

## 1. What a fork reaches

A problem reaches the problems its `from` names. Taken exactly, for a problem F the tracker holds:

- Each `from` an envelope of F's chain names, F's own envelope first and then each ancestor's along `prev`, is a source.
- For a source S the tracker holds, F reaches the line of S: S, and every later version of S the tracker holds, the earlier first. A later version is reached because work posted to it is not on S's own log. Since 11 November 2026 S's map shows that work on a piece S keeps ([reading 2](../v2/READING.md) section 3), so the fork's piece names S there; a later version is where the square stands on a piece S does not keep.
- F reaches as well what S's own chain reaches by the same rule, S's envelope and its ancestors' and not its later versions', so a fork of a fork reaches the problem the first fork came from.

A problem never reaches a version of itself, and a ring of `from` is walked once. A source the tracker does not hold is reached by nothing: the tracker says what it holds, and a peer that pulls the source's chain ([QX-21](QX-21.md)) then says more.

The reach reads every problem the tracker holds, a hidden one among them ([QX-6](QX-6.md)): a hide takes a problem out of a tracker's lists and leaves its record, and an inherited square rests on the record.

## 2. The inherited square

A square stands on a piece, at a problem, where the piece's state there is the problem's own and is one of `accepted_provisional`, `accepted`, `unreachable`, `challenged` or `recorded`. A state a problem inherited is not its own, so a square is read where it was made and never through a fork between.

A piece of F is **inherited** where both hold:

- its own state on F is none of those five and is not `locked`: no set of F's own stands on it, and its own inputs on F are done;
- a square stands on a piece of the same hash at a problem F reaches.

The layer then gives the piece the state `inherited`, and beside it `inherited: { problem, state }`: the problem where the square stands and the piece's state there. Of several squares in reach, the one named is the furthest along, a final one (`accepted`, `unreachable`, `recorded`) over `accepted_provisional` over `challenged`, and of equals the first in the order of section 1. Elsewhere `inherited` is null.

The square is still the source's. A challenge of it is made at the source, where the set is, and the fork's door answers a challenge on a piece with no set of its own as it always has. Where a challenge or the owner's gold strikes the set at the source, the square is gone from every fork at the next read, with no event of its own. A final square is not struck, so what a fork inherits from one stays.

Nothing is credited for an inherited square. Standing ([QX-1](QX-1.md) section 2), stake, the owner's gold and the coin read the sets on a problem's own chain, and an inherited square adds none to the fork's. The roots that made the square have what they had, once, at the source.

The fork's own work is as it was. A claim, a commit, a reveal or a run on an inherited piece is taken by the door, and each reading is kept on the fork's record. Where the fork's own readings make a set, the piece says its own state and `inherited` is null; where they differ, the piece still says `inherited` and the seams between the fork's own readings are shown beside it.

## 3. What waits on it

A piece is **done elsewhere**, for a fork, where a piece of the same hash at a problem the fork reaches has a square that is final: `accepted`, `unreachable` or `recorded` there. A provisional square and a challenged one are shown and are not yet done.

A dependent's input counts as done where the input is `accepted`, `unreachable` or `recorded` on the fork, as before, or where it is done elsewhere and its own inputs on the fork are done. So a piece the fork kept below a piece the fork changed waits for the changed piece, as a kept piece waits across a version in the core ([CORE.md](../CORE.md) section 6), and then its inherited square counts.

The tracker's next never hands over an inherited piece. It hands over a piece that waits on one as it hands over any open piece, and the brief it renders fills the slot of an inherited input with the returns of the accepting set at the problem named, each as quoted text with its signer's address, as the core renders its own ([CORE.md](../CORE.md) section 7). An input recorded elsewhere renders nothing, since there is no agreed return to render ([QX-20](QX-20.md) section 3).

A core client reads an inherited piece as `open` and a piece that waits on it as `locked`. Its map of a fork is coarser than the tracker's and no square on it is wrong: it shows only the sets on the fork's own chain. A client that would take such a piece asks the tracker's next, and renders the brief itself from the source's chain, which it fetches by hash and checks as it checks any.

## 4. Complete, and the record document

A fork is complete, in the tracker's layer, where every piece is `accepted`, `unreachable` or `recorded`, or is `inherited` from a square that is one of those three. `complete` on `GET /p/<hash>/state` and on the index says so. `output` is the core's and stays null on a fork complete by inheritance, as it does on a problem with a piece of a kind the core does not know: the core's output is built from the sets on one chain.

In the record document ([QX-20](QX-20.md) section 4) an inherited piece's entry is `{ name, piece, state: "inherited", inherited }`, where `inherited` is the hash of the problem where the square stands. The readings and the evidence are in that problem's own record and are not copied. A fork's record document is then a function of its own chain and the chains it reaches, and a mirror that holds the same chains writes the same document and the same hash.

The coin's emission and the owner's gold settle on the fork's own sets and its own completion, which inheritance does not move.

## 5. The reference tracker and the client

The tracker computes inheritance last in its layer, after the verifier kinds, the stakes, the golds and the challenges are folded over every problem as they were before. It finds each problem's reach, counts the pieces done elsewhere, and settles the states of the problems that reach something again until nothing moves; then it marks the inherited pieces. A tracker that holds no fork computes what it computed without this extension. The code is `packages/core/src/inherit.ts`, which the core's `read` calls last, and the tracker's layer is that read.

- The well-known document names the extension: `inherit: { extension: "QX-23" }`.
- `GET /p/<hash>/state` gives each piece `inherited`, and the state `inherited` where section 2 says; `counts` on the index counts the state under its own name, and `work.accepted` counts the fork's own squares alone.
- `GET /p/<hash>/next` follows section 3. The order problems are offered in counts an inherited piece as done.
- `GET /p/<hash>/record` follows section 4.

Quilt marks an inherited piece on the map with a mark of its own and says where the square stands: `^` in the strip a model reads, with a line that names each problem and the states the squares have there, and a green ring on the page and the card, full where the square there is final. The page of one square names the problem and links to the same piece's page there. Its own pick leaves out the pieces the tracker says are inherited, and where the tracker hands over a piece the core holds `locked` behind one, Quilt takes it on the tracker's word and renders the brief from the source's chain: it takes the tracker's word that the square there is final, resolves the problem named by its hash, and fills the slot from the accepting set its own fold finds there. Where it cannot read that problem, it does not take the piece. `quilt fork <problem> [<name> ...]` publishes a fork under this home's key: `from` names the problem, its pieces and data are copied as they are, so each keeps its hash and its square, and each proposed piece named ([QX-22](QX-22.md)) is added, by its name, its hash or its event's id, as a take adds one to a version.

## 6. What this draft leaves open

- A square below a changed piece. A piece the fork kept, whose input the fork changed, has its inherited square once the changed input is done, though the square was made over the old input's answer. The core carries a set across a version the same way. A forker who wants the piece read again over the new answer changes its brief, which makes it another piece.
- A tracker's holdings. A tracker that does not hold a source shows the fork's kept pieces `open`, and two trackers that hold different chains show different maps of one fork until the peer pull brings them level. Each map is a reading of what its tracker holds.
- The core's output. A fork complete by inheritance has no `output` document. One that gathers the sets from the chains a fork reaches would be a document of this extension, as the record document is of QX-20.
- Work made again. Nothing stops a fork's workers from making an inherited square again on the fork's own chain, and where they do the fork's own set earns what any set earns. That was so before this extension, and a fork that inherits gives less reason for it.
- A reading that rests on nothing. The layer sets such a reading aside ([QX-19](QX-19.md) section 4) before it reads what a fork inherits, while a piece that waits on an inherited input is still `locked`. So on a piece inheritance opened, two readings that differ both stand, whatever each rests on, and the piece is `disputed` until a third reading agrees with one of them.
- The seams of other kinds. The seams beside an inherited piece are those of an `agree` piece, between the fork's own readings that stand. On an inherited piece of a kind whose readings do not make a set by agreeing, a `human` piece or a judge piece, the reference shows none, since the piece's own state is not kept beside the mark.
- The fork's name. `quilt fork` publishes the fork under the slug the problem had, so where the forker's key already had a problem of that name, the name moves to the fork, as it moves to any problem a key publishes under a name it holds.
- What Quilt takes on the tracker's word. Quilt takes a piece behind an inherited square only where each of the piece's inputs is done in its own fold or inherited from a final square whose set its own fold of the source finds. A piece two steps behind, a source square of a kind the core does not know, and the second reader's pool are left to the map's pick, which holds them `locked`. Quilt does not work out the reach itself: it checks that the problem the tracker names holds a piece of the same hash with an accepting set, which is the same question answered, wherever it stands.
- Complete, in the client. Quilt's `complete` is the core's, so a fork complete by inheritance does not say so in the map a model reads or on the card.
- A fork's standing with its source. An inherited square says nothing of whether the source's owner or workers stand behind the fork. The `from` is the forker's word, as the core takes it.
