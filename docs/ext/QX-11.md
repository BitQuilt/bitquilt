# QX-11: chained ledger lines

Status: Draft. Written 24 October 2026 as PROTOCOL.md's "Chained lines", and first implemented the same day in the reference core, and kept by every store in the repository: the tracker's, a Quilt's and the mirror's. The coin log's head on Ethereum (section 7) was built 27 October 2026. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do. [CORE.md](../CORE.md) section 5 lets a wrapper carry the chain fields of an extension, and section 4 names the identity log's chain. [QX-12](QX-12.md) stamps a chain's head into Bitcoin, [QX-21](QX-21.md) is how doors fold each other's chains, and [NODE.md](../NODE.md) says which program keeps which chain. [COIN.md](../COIN.md) says why the coin has a door out and what it trusts; section 7 says how the door rests on a chain's head.

**Abstract.** A ledger file is never edited or reordered, and the file itself has to show it, with no git around it and no site to ask. So every line a resolver keeps carries its number, the hash of the line before, and its own hash, beginning at the hash of the log it belongs to. A change to any line changes every hash after it, down to the head, and a reader checks a file alone. A chain proves that its keeper has not rewritten or reordered what it served since its head was last seen. It does not prove that the keeper served everything it was sent, or when a line was written. The head is therefore published where the keeper has no hand: the mirror stamps every head into Bitcoin (QX-12), and a bonded poster puts the coin log's head on Ethereum, where it is what the token mints against. A core client ignores the three fields and computes the same map.

## 1. The line

Every receipt a resolver keeps (CORE.md section 5) carries three fields beside its wrapper:

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `seq` | The line's number, counting from 1 with no gap |
| `prev` | The `hash` of the line before; for the first line, the log's hash in hex, the 64 characters after `sha256:` |
| `hash` | SHA-256, in lower-case hex, over the UTF-8 bytes of `seq` in decimal, a newline, `prev`, a newline, and the canonical JSON (CORE.md section 1) of `{ event, received }`: the wrapper without these three fields |

The recipe was first used for the maintainers' public log, with one change: the chain begins at the log, so a chain belongs to one log. `lineHash`, `chainLines` and `nextLine` in `packages/core/src/chain.ts` compute it. The reducer ignores the three fields; a client that reads a file verifies them (section 5).

## 2. The logs

Each log is a chain of its own, begun at its own hash:

- a problem's log, at the problem's hash;
- the identity log, which holds `name`, `delegate`, `revoke` and `rotate` (CORE.md section 4) and the `hide` of QX-6, at the hash of the text `quilt:identity` (`IDENTITY_LOG`, `packages/core/src/events.ts`);
- the coin log (QX-17), at the hash of the text `quilt:coin` (`COIN_LOG`, `packages/tracker/src/coin.ts`).

A tracker serves each log oldest first at `GET /p/<hash>/events`, `GET /k/events` and `GET /coin/events`, one line of JSON a line, after the `seq` its `since` names. The reference tracker serves 500 lines a page and names the next page's `since` in the `x-quilt-next-since` header while more remain; a reader follows the header until it is absent.

A chain is a resolver's own. Two trackers can hold the same events in different orders under different chains. A reader that reads both verifies each file and merges the events by id, by the union of their wrappers (CORE.md section 5).

## 3. Appending

- **A line received.** The reference tracker stamps it with a time strictly after the last line on the same log, so two events on one log never share a time and a commit always folds before its reveal (`Store.appendReceipt`).
- **A line pulled.** An event taken from a peer's chain keeps the wrappers it came with and gains the puller's own, and nothing is stamped: the earliest wrapper decides its time in every fold (QX-21 section 2, `Store.appendPulled`). The mirror and a node with `keep` append the same way (NODE.md section 5).
- **A line kept as given.** A chain kept where it only grew the one held keeps the other place's `seq`, `prev` and `hash` (`appendLines`): a Quilt that follows one place's chain does this, and so does the mirror with the coin log, which it copies whole.

## 4. The head

A chain's head is its last line's `seq` and `hash`, and a short form: the first sixteen hex characters of the hash, as two groups of eight joined by a colon (`shortHead`). The tracker states it on each problem's index row and in its scrape (`GET /p/<hash>/scrape`), with `chain_ok` saying whether the chain verifies whole, and bitquilt.org's problems page shows its `seq` and short form as the problem's latest receipt (`site/problems.html`). The mirror writes no head file, since the head is the ledger file's last line, and keeps the head's OpenTimestamps file under `anchors/<problem>/<seq>.ots` (QX-12 section 2).

## 5. Reading a chain

`verifyChain(lines, log)` checks a file from its first line: each `seq` one more than the last, each `prev` the hash before, each `hash` its own. It names the first line that fails and returns the head. `onlyAppends(before, now)` says whether a chain read now only grew the one held: every earlier line is the same line in the same place.

Every keeper uses both. A chain that does not verify is not taken, by a door's pull (QX-21 section 2), by the mirror, or by a Quilt's keep. A chain that did not only grow is not kept where one place's chain is followed: a Quilt's keep, and the coin log at the mirror. A chain rewritten whole, with every hash recomputed, verifies, and still fails `onlyAppends` against what was held.

## 6. What a chain proves, and what it does not

A chain proves that its keeper has not rewritten, reordered or removed what it served since a reader last saw its head.

- It does not prove completeness. An event a tracker declined to receive, or never served, leaves no gap.
- It does not prove time. The keeper stamps the times, and could rewrite a whole chain from a point and stamp it again for a reader who saw no later head. Publishing the head where the keeper has no hand closes that: QX-12 stamps every head the mirror holds into Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps, and section 7 puts the coin log's head on Ethereum.
- It does not make an event valid. The door and the reducer decide that; the chain says what the keeper received, and in what order.

## 7. The coin log's head on Ethereum

The coin leaves the ledger by a `redeem` line and comes back by a deposit that the bridge's reader credits (QX-17 sections 2 and 4). Both rest on the coin log's head, posted to the chain under a bond. [COIN.md](../COIN.md), "The door out" and "Trust, said plainly", says why there is a door out and what it trusts. Two contracts in `contracts/src` carry it: `Anchors` holds the heads, and `Quilt` mints a redeem once its proof reaches a head's redeem root, which it checks with the `Merkle` library (`contracts/src/Merkle.sol`), the same recipe as `packages/coin/src/merkle.ts` (section 7.1). Four commands in `packages/coin` run it. Only `packages/coin/src/chain.ts` imports the RPC library (`viem`), and `packages/coin/test/tripwire.test.ts` holds it there (AGENTS.md, rule 10). The protocol reads no chain: these jobs and the mirror do.

### 7.1 The redeem root

A redeem line's leaf is `keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32 id, address to, uint256 amount))`. `id` is the coin log line's `hash` (section 1); `to` is the address the redeem names, or the root's own; `amount` is in units, a million to a coin. The leaves are sorted ascending. A pair is hashed with the smaller first, `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(a, b))`, so a proof is a list of siblings with no side bits. An odd node at a level is carried up unchanged. The root of no leaves is thirty-two zero bytes, and the root of one leaf is the leaf. `contracts/src/Merkle.sol` and `packages/coin/src/merkle.ts` are one recipe, held to one fixture on each side (`contracts/test/Merkle.t.sol`, `packages/coin/test/merkle.test.ts`).

### 7.2 Anchors

`Anchors` (`contracts/src/Anchors.sol`) holds, per sequence, the coin log's head (the line's `hash` as `bytes32`), the redeem root over every redeem line at or before it, when it was posted and by whom. It is deployed with the veto address, the treasury, the least bond a poster holds (`minBond`, in wei) and the veto window (`vetoWindow`, in seconds). The window is how long the veto has to strike a false head, and so how long a holder waits between a head's post and a claim under it. The maintainers deploy it from a wallet they hold ([DEPLOY.md](../DEPLOY.md), "The bridge", step 1), with a window of a week, 604800 seconds, as the coin vests (`vesting_h` is 168, QX-17 section 1).

- `bond()` adds to the sender's bond. A poster holding at least `minBond` is `allowed`.
- `post(seq, head, redeemRoot)` is an allowed poster's. The sequence goes up, except that a vetoed sequence may be posted again.
- `veto(seq, reason)` is the veto address's alone, inside the window, once: the post is struck and the poster's whole bond goes to the treasury. The veto can do nothing else.
- `isFinal(seq)` is true for a post that stands and is past its window. `headAt(seq)` reads a post.
- `withdraw(amount)` returns bond once none of the poster's posts is inside the window.

A posted head is bonded, not proven. Anyone can check it against the head the mirror stamped (QX-12), and the veto strikes a false one inside the window.

### 7.3 Quilt

`Quilt` (`contracts/src/Quilt.sol`) is an ERC-20 with six decimals, so that a base unit is a unit of the ledger's coin. Nobody owns it, and nothing mints it but `redeem(seq, id, to, amount, proof)`: the head at `seq` is final, the leaf of `(id, to, amount)` stands under its redeem root by the proof, and `id` has not been redeemed before, so each line mints once. `deposit(amount, root)` burns the sender's coin for the ledger to credit to `root`, numbers the deposit from 1 and keeps it (`depositAt(n)`). The chain's supply stays at or below what the ledger redeemed less what came back.

### 7.4 The jobs

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `bitquilt-poster` (`post.ts`) | Reads `GET /coin/redeems` from the coin's home: the head, and every redeem line under it. Builds the tree, and posts the head and the root to `Anchors` when the home's head is newer than the last posted. Run again over the same head, it posts nothing. |
| `bitquilt-claim <line id>` (`claim.ts`) | The holder's. Reads the last posted sequence and the home's redeem lines up to it, and checks the posted head and root against them. Refuses while the head is inside its window, and for a line redeemed already. Then calls `Quilt.redeem` with the proof, the holder paying the gas once. |
| `bitquilt-deposit <amount> [root]` (`deposit-bin.ts`) | The holder's. Burns that many units on the chain for the ledger root named, the holder's own address when none is. |
| `bitquilt-reader` (`read.ts`) | Reads the deposits as of the chain's finalized block and the credits the home holds (`GET /coin/credits`). Posts a signed `credit` on the coin log for each deposit not yet credited, in order. |

The reader signs with the poster's key, bonded in `Anchors`; the coin's parameters name it as `reader` (QX-17 section 1). The ledger admits a credit on the reader's signature alone. The mirror checks every credit against `depositAt` and keeps no balances where one differs (NODE.md section 6), and a false credit is answered by vetoing the reader's post, which takes the bond. [DEPLOY.md](../DEPLOY.md), "The bridge", says how the contracts are deployed and the jobs run, and `.env.example` lists what each reads.

## 8. The reference implementation

[AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. These are the functions that keep the chain.

- The core's `lineHash`, `chainLines`, `nextLine`, `verifyChain`, `onlyAppends` and `shortHead`.
- The tracker's store: `appendReceipt`, `appendPulled`, `appendLines`, `lines` (the whole log) and `head`; the tracker's views serve the logs a page at a time (`events`), the scrape's head and `chain_ok`, and say `chain: "QX-11"` in the well-known document.
- The client's resolver: `receipts` and `keep`, which read a chain, verify it and keep it where it only grew.
- The mirror's `takeChain`, which appends the events a source's chain has and the store lacks, in that source's order, with the source's wrappers kept and the keeper's own added, or copies the coin log whole. Which source a problem is taken from in a run is [NODE.md](../NODE.md) section 5.
- The contracts and the coin's jobs, for section 7.

- `packages/core/test/events.test.ts`, "chained lines": a chain from the problem verifies, with its head's short form, the hash's first sixteen hex characters as two groups of eight, and a line appended; a changed, reordered or removed line is caught; and a chain rewritten whole verifies and fails `onlyAppends`.
- `packages/tracker/test/tracker.test.ts`, "stamps receipts strictly increasing, chains them, and refuses a duplicate": the scrape states the head as `verifyChain` reads it from the served chain, its short form included.
- `packages/tracker/test/long-log.test.ts`: every read of a whole log reads past the store's old page of ten thousand lines.
- `packages/mirror/test/mirror.test.ts`: chains that verify, a chain that only grows, a second tracker's events appended after the lines held, and pages with no end. `sources.test.ts`: the source each log is taken from.
- `packages/client/test/keep.test.ts`: a Quilt keeps one place's chain and reads another's without keeping it, and keeps one place's identity log.
- `contracts/test/Anchors.t.sol`, `Quilt.t.sol` and `Merkle.t.sol` (`cd contracts && forge test`), and `packages/coin/test`: the tree, the poster, the claim, the reader and the tripwire. `anvil.test.ts` runs the door out on a local chain where Foundry is installed, and skips without it.
