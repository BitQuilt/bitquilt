# QX-22: proposed pieces

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 10 November 2026 in the reference tracker and the Quilt client; [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [QX-20](QX-20.md) is the record kind, whose way of keeping every reading side by side this follows, and [QX-19](QX-19.md) section 4 is the owner's sharpening, which publishes a version as a take does. A core client ignores all of it: it passes over a `propose` event as it does any type it does not know, reads a version that took a proposed piece as it reads any version, and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** A pattern is written before the work, and the work finds what the pattern missed: a question under the question, a case no piece covers, a source nobody listed. In the core only the owner adds a piece, and only what the owner thought of. This extension lets any key put one piece beside a problem with a signed `propose` event: a name, the piece as a pattern writes one, and the reason for it. Proposed pieces stand as a record. Each is kept side by side with every other, two under one name among them; nothing accepts one or strikes one, no standing is credited and no coin is earned. A proposed piece joins the map where the owner takes it into a version of the problem, which keeps every square the problem has, and the owner's node takes one by default where its proposer has work standing on the problem and the budget has room. A fork may hold what the owner leaves. The tracker says of each proposed piece which of these became of it, and the reducer never sees the event.

## 1. The event

One event type, on the problem's log.

| Type | Names | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propose` | `problem` and no `piece` | `{ name, piece, why }` | `name` is a slug by the rule the envelope's check holds a problem's slug to, lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, at most 64. `piece` is an object, one piece as a pattern writes one: it has no `count`, since a proposal is one piece and not a family, and no `data`, since a proposed piece draws on no data. `piece` is at most 16,384 bytes as canonical JSON. A proposed piece does not wait on itself: its `inputs` do not name `name`. `piece.brief`, where it is text, passes the plain-text gate (CORE.md section 11). `why` is the proposer's reason, text of at most 500 characters through the plain-text gate. The body has no other field. The piece is one its hash can name, and the event is signed by its own key. |

Any key may propose, the owner's among them. A `propose` is a post like any other and meets the limits every post meets, the key's hourly limit and the address's. It asks no stake and no standing, and it earns none, and no coin.

A proposal names the version it was made under, in the event's `problem`. The door takes one under a version its owner has since replaced, since the proposals of every version of a line stand at its head (section 2).

A proposed piece is named by the hash it has in any envelope that takes it under its name. A single piece's hash reads its name and its own fields and nothing of the problem around it (CORE.md section 3), so the hash is had from the body alone: the piece set alone under `name` and expanded. A piece that cannot be expanded so has no hash and is no proposal.

The door holds the piece to every rule a version that took it would be held to at publish, with room made for the one piece in the budget, which is the owner's to give. A name the problem has for a piece or a family is refused first. Then the door drafts the version, the one the event names with the piece added under its name and the budget one higher, and asks of the draft what it asks of a version: the nesting, asked of the version that would take the piece, since a piece sits a level deeper in an envelope than in the event that proposes it; then the envelope's own check (CORE.md section 2); then the door's bounds on a version's hours, names, pieces, inputs, expansion and chain. A fault of the proposed piece is said of `body.piece`. The verifier is any kind the envelope's check passes, so a kind this tracker does not implement can be proposed, as one can be published.

A `propose` that fails the table is passed over by a reader, as the core drops an event, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason. The draft's faults are the door's refusals and not rules of validity, as the gate at publish is admission and not validity (CORE.md section 11).

## 2. What stands

The proposals that stand on a problem are the well-formed `propose` events on its chain, in receipt order. Of several proposals of one piece by one root, the first stands and the rest are passed over.

Well-formed is section 1's table: the body's shape, a piece its hash can name, and the signature of the event's own key. These are read from the line alone, so a reader of a chain it did not admit, a clone or a peer's lines, counts the same proposals as the door that took them.

A root is the key's root when the proposal was received (CORE.md section 6), so two keys of one root propose a piece once. Two roots that propose one piece are two proposals, and two pieces under one name stand side by side, each under its own hash. The door refuses a proposal this root already made of this piece, `this root already proposed this piece`, since the read would pass it over.

The chain a version is read over holds its own receipts and its ancestors', so the proposals of every version of a line stand at its head. A read as of a time counts the proposals received by then. Nothing strikes a proposal and nothing withdraws one.

## 3. Taken, pursued and worked

The tracker says three things of each proposal that stands.

- `worked` is whether the proposer's root has work standing on the problem: a root of an accepting set on a piece of the problem or of an ancestor, or of a reading that stands on a record piece ([QX-20](QX-20.md) section 2). The tracker reads it over its layer, so a set a held challenge or the owner's gold struck is not work that stands, and a set of any kind the tracker decides is.
- `taken` is the first version after the one the proposal names, up to the version read, whose envelope holds a piece of that hash, or null where none does. A read at the version a proposal names says null.
- `pursued` is the forks of a version of the line, as this tracker lists them, whose envelopes hold a piece of that hash. A fork of the line is a problem another owner published with `from` naming a version of the line, and each later version of that fork, so a fork is found over its own chain and its head is the one listed. A fork this tracker has taken down ([QX-6](QX-6.md)) or its owner has replaced is not listed.

Taken and pursued are found by the hash and not by the name: a version that holds the same piece under the same name took it, whoever wrote it there, and a version that holds another piece under that name did not.

Each of the three is the tracker's reading of what it holds, and the proposal itself is the signed event. Another tracker, holding other forks, says another `pursued`; a clone recomputes all three from the chains it has.

## 4. The owner's take

A proposal waits while no version after the one it names, up to the head, holds its piece.

A take publishes a version: the head's pieces with the taken pieces added under their names, and `prev` naming the head. A version changes no existing piece's hash, so every square the problem has stands on the new version (CORE.md section 6). A piece taken is a piece like any other from then on: `next` hands it out and its kind accepts it, and its proposer earns nothing by having proposed it.

The owner's node takes a waiting proposal by default when its proposer's root is the owner's own or has work standing on the problem, its name is free at the head, and the budget has room for it, in receipt order. The default asks nothing of anyone. It leaves a piece whose verifier kind the client does not implement, and says why it left each piece it left.

A take by name takes any waiting proposal and raises the budget to fit. The name given is the proposal's name, the piece's hash or the `propose` event's id; a name takes the first that waits under it. Either way a piece is held to the version that would take it, so one that no longer fits the head is left with the fault, and a name the head holds is not taken under.

The budget is the room a problem has to grow: an owner who sets it above the pattern's pieces leaves that many places for what the work finds, and one who sets it to the count leaves none, and takes by name. The reference client publishes a pattern that names no budget with one of 1000.

The client reads the proposals that stand from the signed lines of the chain it resolved, by section 2's rule, and reads the work that stands over the core's fold of that chain: the roots of each accepting set, and of the readings that stand on a record piece by [QX-20](QX-20.md) section 2's rule. A piece two roots proposed is added once, and both proposals are said taken.

The version is published as a sharpening's is ([QX-19](QX-19.md) section 4): signed with the home's key, posted with the topics of the owner's announce, the gold the home holds for the problem committed again on the new version ([QX-16](QX-16.md)), and the home noting which version replaced which. A problem the owner froze gets no version. A problem its owner already replaced is left as it is. Only the owner takes: a version follows `prev` under one owner.

The take is a step of the owner's turn, after the sharpening, so it reads the version the sharpening left. A home's node runs the turn every hour, and the hosted on-ramp runs it for the owners whose keys it holds. The turn says nothing of a problem where nothing was taken and nothing refused, so a proposal left waiting is not said again every hour.

## 5. The reference tracker and the client

- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `propose: { extension: "QX-22", piece_max: 16384, why_max: 500 }`.
- `POST /p/<hash>/events` takes a `propose` by section 1. Its refusals are `400` with the reason: `a proposal names a problem and no piece`, the first fault of the body's shape, `body.name: this problem has a piece named` and the name, or `a family named`, the first fault of the draft version, `body.piece: a piece its hash can name`, and `this root already proposed this piece`.
- `GET /p/<hash>/proposals` gives `{ problem, now, proposals }`: the proposals that stand over the chain of the version asked, in receipt order, each with `id` (the event's id), `under` (the version it names), `name`, `hash`, `piece`, `why`, `key`, `root`, `at` (its receipt's time), `proposer` (the name the root holds here, or null), and section 3's `worked`, `taken` and `pursued`. A problem taken down still answers its own.
- A peer's `propose` line is pulled through the same check as a post ([QX-21](QX-21.md) section 2).
- `quilt propose <problem> <name> <piece.json> <why>` and the tool `quilt_propose` sign and post a proposal under the head. The client holds the body to section 1 before it signs, in the door's words and order, and refuses a second proposal of one piece from its own root. The answer names the piece's hash.
- `quilt proposals <problem>` and `quilt_read` say the proposed pieces, each with its name, its hash, its proposer and whether the proposer's work stands, whether a version took it or it waits, the forks that pursue it, and its kind and how strongly that kind earns a square. The proposer's reason and brief are another person's words: each is quoted with the proposer named, and one that does not pass the plain-text gate is counted and not shown. The tool's handles list each under `proposed` by its event's id, its piece's hash and the version that took it, with none of the proposer's words. `quilt proposals --json` prints the list as the tracker gives it.
- `quilt take` runs the default over every problem the home owns, `quilt take <problem>` over one, and `quilt take <problem> <name> ...` takes by name. There is no tool for a take: the default runs in the owner's turn, and a take by name is the command line's.
- The phrase `propose_piece` is the sentence a person says to have their AI draft a piece a pattern lacks and propose it. An away run is not offered the tool that proposes.

## 6. What this draft leaves open

- A cap. Nothing bounds the proposals on a problem or from one root but the limits every post meets.
- Families. A proposal is one piece: a family, or a piece that draws on the problem's data, cannot be proposed.
- A decline. The owner has no mark for a proposal it will not take, and one left waiting says nothing of why.
- Work, read two ways. The tracker reads the work that stands over its layer and the client's default over the core's fold. A set a held challenge or the owner's gold struck still counts in the client's reading, and a set of a kind the core does not know, `judge`, `human` or `attest`, does not.
- Stake rules. A stake rule ([QX-15](QX-15.md)) is read on the version it names, so the version a take publishes has none until its owner posts one, as a sharpening's has none.
- A fork's squares. A fork starts empty in the core, so a fork that pursues a proposed piece carries over none of the squares of the problem it forks, and a key that is not the owner has no take. A later extension may let a fork inherit the squares of the pieces it shares.
- The page and the card. Neither shows proposed pieces yet; the tool, the command line and the tracker's route do.
