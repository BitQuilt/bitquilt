# QX-17: the coin as a layer

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 26 October 2026 in the reference tracker, the mirror and the Quilt client, amended the same day and the next (section 8). [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. Transfers are on from the home's genesis and redemption from the day the bridge stands, by the founder's decisions of 27 October 2026. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [COIN.md](../COIN.md) says what the coin is for and what it is not; QX-15 gives the stake it is locked as, QX-16 the verified square it is minted for, QX-11 and QX-12 the chain and the anchor its log has. A core client ignores all of it and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** A tracker may carry a coin as a layer over its ledger, as QX-1 carries standing. The coin's own events live on one log of their own, the coin log, chained and anchored like a problem's; the squares that earn it and the strikes that slash it are read from the problem logs the same tracker holds. A fixed amount is minted each epoch and divided among the squares verified in it; what is minted vests with time; a root locks coin at a tracker's door to back a claim and loses it when the claim's square is struck; a payer escrows coin a problem's workers are paid from; a root burns coin for standing. Every balance is a function of the receipts and the published parameters, computed by the tracker, by the mirror and by anyone with the receipts; the tracker orders the lines and decides nothing. One tracker is the coin's home, and another that honours the coin reads the home's ledger at its door. Nobody needs a wallet, a gas token or a node to earn, hold, lock, pay or be paid.

## 1. The parameters

A tracker that carries the coin publishes its parameters in its well-known document under `coin`, and they are the coin's constitution: a reader computes balances from them, the mirror checks balances against them, and a change to any of them is a new version of the layer every reader can see.

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `extension` | `QX-17` |
| `home` | `here`, or the address of the tracker whose ledger this one reads |
| `genesis` | The time epochs count from |
| `epoch_h` | The length of an epoch in hours |
| `emission` | `{ per_epoch, decline_bp, period_epochs, floor_per_epoch?, max_per_weight? }`: the units minted in an epoch of the first period, and the basis points by which the amount falls after every `period_epochs` epochs. `floor_per_epoch`, where present, is the tail: the units an epoch's amount never falls below. `max_per_weight`, where present, caps what an epoch mints at that many units a unit of verified weight; the rest of the curve's amount is unminted (28 October 2026) |
| `vesting_h` | The hours over which a minting vests; the home's draft is a week (decided 27 October 2026; a year before), longer than a launch pattern's challenge window so a strike can still take back what a fake minted |
| `infra_bp` | The basis points of each epoch's minting that go to the treasury |
| `treasury` | The root that receives them |
| `unit` | How many units make a coin; every amount in the layer is in units, whole |
| `weights` | `{ cross_vendor, same_vendor, attest }`: a verified square's weight in its epoch, by how independent the set that made it was |
| `burn_rate` | The units of coin a point of standing costs at this tracker's door; with `burn_indexed`, the floor under the rate in force |
| `burn_indexed` | `true` where a point costs what a reveal in a verified cross-vendor square earned in the latest ended epoch (the epoch's payout a unit of weight times `weights.cross_vendor`), never less than `burn_rate`; absent or false, `burn_rate` is the rate (28 October 2026) |
| `window_max_h` | The longest `challenge_window_h` of a problem the home admits, so that a strike inside any window finds the square's coin still unvested; absent, no bound (28 October 2026) |
| `transfers_from`, `redeem_from` | The times from which `transfer` and `redeem` are kept, or `null` for never yet. The home tracker names its genesis as `transfers_from`, and as `redeem_from` once the bridge's contracts stand (the founder's decisions of 27 October 2026) |
| `reader` | The root whose `credit` lines are kept: the bridge's reader, the same key as its poster and bonded in `Anchors` with it; absent or null, no credit is kept |

## 2. The coin log

A pseudo-problem, `quilt:coin`, hashed as the identity log is, chained under QX-11 from its hash and anchored under QX-12 like any head. The tracker takes its events at `POST /coin/events` and serves them at `GET /coin/events?since=<seq>`. An event on it names no `problem` or `piece` in the event's own fields, as the identity log's do not; what it is about is in its body. An event's id is its identifier everywhere below.

| Type | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lock` | `{ problem, piece, amount }` | `problem` is one this tracker holds and the core folds (CORE.md section 6), and `piece` is one of its pieces; `amount` is a whole number of at least 1; the root's **free** coin at the receipt covers `amount`. The lock backs a stake of `floor(amount / rate)` standing, at the rate of section 4 (section 5). |
| `pay` | `{ problem, amount, per_square }` | `problem` is one this tracker holds and the core folds; `amount` and `per_square` are whole numbers of at least 1; the root's **free vested** coin at the receipt covers `amount`, which is escrowed to the problem (section 4). |
| `burn` | `{ amount }` | A whole number of at least 1; the root's free coin at the receipt covers it. The coin is gone, and the tracker credits `floor(amount / rate)` standing, at the rate of section 4 (QX-15). |
| `transfer` | `{ to, amount }` | Received at or after `transfers_from`; `to` is an address; the root's free vested coin covers `amount`. |
| `redeem` | `{ amount, to? }` | Received at or after `redeem_from`; `to`, where given, is an address; the root's free vested coin covers `amount`. The coin leaves the ledger, and the line is what the chain mints to `to`, or to the root, once, by the line's own hash: the door out ([COIN.md](../COIN.md), The door out). |
| `credit` | `{ deposit, root, amount }` | The way back: the signer's root is the `reader` the parameters name, `deposit` is a whole number from 1 not credited before, `root` is an address and `amount` a whole number of at least 1. The ledger takes the reader's word at the door; the mirror checks every credit against the deposit the chain holds at that number, and refuses the balances where one differs (section 7). |

A delegate signs any of them only with it in `scope`. An event that fails a rule is dropped silently, as the core drops, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason.

## 3. What the layer derives from the problem logs

From the problem logs the tracker holds, as QX-1, QX-15 and QX-16 fold them, the layer derives:

| Line | From | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `verified` (problem, piece, root, weight, at) | a QX-16 match, at the reveal's receipt, one per root of the accepting set; the weight is `cross_vendor` where the set's reveals declare different vendors, `same_vendor` where they do not, `attest` for an attest set | emission |
| `forfeit` and `restore` (coin lock, at) | the QX-15 lock a coin lock is bound to, forfeited or restored | slashing |
| `release` (coin lock, at) | the QX-15 lock released, or the coin lock left unbound past the piece's lease | freeing a lock |
| `accepted` (problem, piece, roots, at) | each accepting set as the layer's fold stands, at its completion, with the roots `creditedRoots` gives: the reveals, not the runs | payments |
| `complete` (problem, at) | the problem complete, at the latest window close among its pieces | refunds |
| `mint` (epoch, at) | the end of every epoch at or before now | emission |

The coin log's lines and the derived lines are ordered together by time, derived lines before posted ones at the same instant and ties by id, which is deterministic from the receipts: anyone holding them computes the same balances, and the mirror does.

## 4. The rules

- **Emission.** Epoch `n` covers the hours from `genesis + n × epoch_h`. The curve's amount, the epoch's **ceiling**, is `per_epoch`, reduced `floor(n / period_epochs)` times by `decline_bp` basis points, each reduction floored, and never below `floor_per_epoch` where that is set. The epoch's **amount** is the ceiling, or where `max_per_weight` is set the lesser of the ceiling and `max_per_weight` times the epoch's verified weight: a unit of weight earns at most that, and the rest of the ceiling is unminted. At the epoch's end, `floor(amount × infra_bp / 10000)` goes to the treasury and the rest is divided among the epoch's `verified` lines pro rata by weight, each share floored; the remainder is unminted. An epoch with no verified line mints nothing, to nobody, and nothing is carried.
- **Vesting.** A root's vested total at a time is the sum over its mintings of each amount scaled by how much of `vesting_h` has passed since it, floored, plus everything it received by payment or transfer. Coin spent by a `transfer` or a `redeem` is drawn from the vested part; coin taken by a forfeit, a `burn` or a `pay` is drawn from the unvested part first (a `pay` drew on the vested part alone until 27 October 2026, when the founder asked that a key put up a bounty with what it just earned). A root's **vested** coin is its vested total less what was drawn from it, bounded by its balance; its **unvested** coin is the rest. Coin taken from the unvested part does not slow the vesting of what remains: this is a coarsening, named in section 8.
- **Balance, free, free vested.** A root's **balance** is what it was minted and received less what it spent, burned, forfeited, transferred and redeemed. Locked and escrowed coin is part of the balance, set aside. **Free** is the balance less locked less escrowed; **free vested** is free or vested, whichever is less.
- **Locks.** A coin lock is live from its receipt. It is **bound** when a QX-15 lock of kind `coin` names it, and then ends as that lock ends: released with it, forfeited with it, restored with it. Left unbound, it is released at its receipt plus the piece's lease. A forfeit removes the lock's coin and as much again from the root's unvested coin as there is; a restore returns both.
- **Payments.** A `pay` escrows its amount to the problem from free coin, vested or not. Each `accepted` on that problem after it pays `per_square` from the earliest escrow with coin left, divided equally among the set's credited roots, each share floored and the remainder staying in the escrow; a `complete` refunds every escrow's remainder to its payer. Received coin is vested.
- **Burn.** Removes the coin, from the unvested part first, and the tracker credits `floor(amount / rate)` standing to the root from the receipt.
- **The rate.** The units a point of standing costs at a receipt are `burn_rate`, or with `burn_indexed` the greater of `burn_rate` and `floor((minted − treasury) / weight) × weights.cross_vendor` of the latest epoch ended by the receipt, which is what one reveal in a verified cross-vendor square earned there; an epoch with no verified weight leaves the floor. The same rate backs a coin-kind lock (section 5) and prices a `burn`. Money then buys the record of work at about the price work earns it (28 October 2026).
- **Transfers and redemption** are kept only from the times the parameters name, and draw on free vested coin. A `redeem` removes the coin from the ledger for good; the line is what the chain mints against, once, by the line's own hash ([COIN.md](../COIN.md), the door out).
- **Credits.** A `credit` adds its amount to the root's received coin, vested, as a transfer's is. One credit stands per deposit number.
- **Arithmetic** is in whole units, and nothing rounds in anyone's favour: a remainder stays where it was.

## 5. Stake at the door, with coin

Under QX-15 a `lock { kind: "coin", amount, id }` on a problem's log names a coin lock by `id`, with `amount` the stake in standing. The tracker admits it when the coin lock is the root's, names this problem and piece, is live and unbound, and backs at least `amount`: `floor(units / rate) ≥ amount`, at the rate of section 4 in force at the home. At its home the tracker reads its own coin layer; elsewhere it reads `GET <home>/coin/lock/<id>`, which carries the rate, and in either case it writes what it read into the receipt's wrapper as the resolver's word: `stake: { kind: "coin", amount, id, units, home, head }`. The QX-15 layer folds a coin-kind lock on that word, as a reader takes `custody`; the coin layer binds the coin lock to it. A coin-kind lock counts in no root's locked standing; a tracker that carries no coin and reads no home refuses the kind.

## 6. The reference tracker

- Carries the coin when its options name the parameters, as `QUILT_COIN` names them for the binary ([DEPLOY.md](../DEPLOY.md)); otherwise it has no coin log and refuses a coin-kind lock with `this tracker honours no coin lock`.
- Refuses at the door, with the reason: `the coin log takes lock, pay, burn, transfer, redeem and credit`, `a coin event names no problem in its fields`, `no such problem here`, `the core refuses this problem as held here: ` with the core's reason, `names a piece this problem does not have`, `free coin M is below the lock of N`, `free coin M is below the pay of N`, `free coin M is below the burn of N`, `transfers are off`, `free vested coin M is below the transfer of N`, `redemption is off`, `free vested coin M is below the redeem of N`, `this ledger names no reader`, `the reader alone credits a deposit`, `deposit N is credited already`; and for a coin-kind QX-15 lock: `no such lock on the coin log`, `the lock is another root's`, `the lock names another piece`, `the lock is bound`, `the lock has ended`, `the lock holds U units, a stake of S at R a point`, `the home did not answer`.
- At the coin's home, admits a problem only where its `challenge_window_h` is at most `window_max_h` when that is set, refusing with `the challenge window of W hours is beyond the H this home admits (QX-17)`.
- `GET /coin/lock/<id>`: the lock, its root, problem, piece, units, receipt, whether it is bound and to what, its state: `live`, `released`, `forfeited`, and `rate`, the burn rate in force.
- `GET /coin/balance/<root>`: balance, vested, unvested, locked, escrowed, free, free vested, minted, received, burned, forfeited, redeemed, at the head.
- `GET /coin/redeems?upto=<seq>`: the redeem lines kept, each with the line's hash as its `id`, its sequence, root, `to` and amount, under the head at `upto` or the current one: what the bridge's poster puts in its tree.
- `GET /coin/credits?upto=<seq>`: the credit lines kept, each with its deposit number, root and amount, and the reader's address: what the bridge's reader has posted and the mirror checks.
- `GET /coin/epochs?since=<n>`: each ended epoch's number, bounds, ceiling, amount, treasury share, verified weight and what was minted.
- `GET /coin/state`: the head and every balance, for the mirror, with `folded`, what the balances were folded over: `at`, the time the layer under them was folded at, which is the state's own `at` or up to five seconds before it, since the tracker keeps its layer that long while nothing is written and computes the coin at its clock's time held to `folded.at` and the five seconds after it, however long the fold took; `problems`, each problem the layer folded, every one the tracker holds but one whose input the core refuses, in the order it folded them, each as `{ problem, head }` with its chain's head as `{ seq, hash }`, or null for a chain with no line; `refused`, each problem the tracker holds whose input the core refuses, which has no state (CORE.md section 6), each as `{ problem, head }` too, since a later version's fold reads an earlier one's chain; and `identity`, the identity log's head. With the coin log to the state's `head`, these are every chain the fold reads, and the tracker serves each by hash, a hidden problem's (QX-6 section 3) and a replaced one's among them. The rest of what the fold reads is published too: the parameters and the founding standing in the well-known document, the envelopes and data blobs at `/p/<hash>` and `/b/<hash>`, and the reads a trace names at `/b/<hash>`, which the fold measures by their bytes (QX-19 section 2). The tracker holds every data blob of a problem it folds, since the core refuses a problem without them, and the read of every trace on a line it wrote, since its door takes no trace whose read it lacks; it may lack a data blob that only problems the core refuses name; a node with `work` beside the door keeps chains the door did not take ([NODE.md](../NODE.md) section 2), and the fold measures a read it lacks as absent, for which `/b/<hash>` answers `404`. So a reader who folds those chains as the tracker serves them, cut at those heads, the layer at `folded.at` and the coin at `at`, refuses the same problems and computes the same balances.
- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `coin: { ...the parameters of section 1 }` and, under QX-15's `stake`, `coin: "here"` or the home's address and `burn_rate`, the rate in force now.

## 7. The mirror

The mirror pulls the coin log into `ledger/coin.jsonl`, verifies its chain from the coin log's hash and that it only grew, and anchors its head as `anchors/coin/<seq>` beside the problems'. It reads `/coin/state` after the tracker's list, its problems' chains and its identity log, and before the coin log, so the log it takes holds every line the state names. The state must agree with what the mirror already read: it names every problem the tracker listed or served in that run, and names none it listed as refused, since its index lists no problem the core refuses, nor one the mirror holds that folds over what the mirror holds, since copies the mirror holds refute it, it names each chain the mirror read at a head no earlier than the lines read and on those lines or on a chain that extends them, since a chain only grows, and it was folded at its own `at` or up to five seconds before it. The mirror then computes the layer itself over what the state says the balances were folded over (section 6): each chain the state names, the coin log's, the identity log's and each problem's, as the tracker serves it, read whole, verified from its log's hash and cut at the named head, whose line must carry the named hash, with the parameters and the founding standing the tracker publishes, and each data blob and each read a trace names as the tracker serves it, a data blob the tracker answers it does not hold left out where only problems the state names as refused name it and the mirror holds no copy of it, since a tracker that withholds a blob and one that lacks it look the same, each read measured by its bytes and a read the tracker answers it does not hold left out where no line the tracker wrote names it, so it sets aside what the tracker sets aside (QX-19 section 4). It folds the tracker's chains and not its own, since its own lines carry its own hashes and would draw other panels (QX-1 section 4). It compares every balance with the tracker's: where they agree it writes `coin/balances.json`; where they do not it writes nothing and reports the head as one it cannot reproduce; and where the state is not what it already read, a chain, an envelope, a data blob or a read the state rests on is not served as named, or the core refuses here other problems than the state names as refused, it refuses the balances, since they then rest on receipts the tracker does not serve as it says. Where the log holds a credit, it writes the balances only where every credit is what the chain holds at that deposit (QX-11 section 7), and refuses them where one is not; with no chain to ask, it writes none and refuses nothing for it ([NODE.md](../NODE.md) section 6). A balance the mirror holds is therefore one it recomputed from receipts the tracker serves to anyone, cut where the tracker said it folded them, and the coin log under it has its head stamped in Bitcoin.

## 8. What this version does not do

- **One home.** The coin is as decentralized as the tracker that orders its log; the mirror's copy and the anchor bound that, and the door out ([COIN.md](../COIN.md)) is the later answer.
- **Vesting is per root, not per lot.** Coin taken from the unvested part does not slow the vesting of what remains, which favours a slashed root a little; a reader that wants lots can compute them from the same receipts, and a later version may say so.
- **A payer's coin waits for completion.** An escrow on a problem that never completes is never refunded; a payer chooses what to pay for.
- **An escrow stays on its version.** A `pay` names one problem, and a version of it is another problem to the coin. A set made at one version and carried to a later one is accepted on each, and each version's escrow pays it, as each payer chose. A set made at a later version alone is not paid from an earlier version's escrow, and once the work has moved to a later version the earlier one may never complete, so its escrow waits as above. The owner's gold verifies a carried square once, at the version that holds the gold ([QX-1](QX-1.md) section 2, amended 10 November 2026). Whether an escrow should follow a problem's line is for a later version of this extension.
- **The reader's word.** A credit is admitted on the reader's signature; the door reads no chain. The mirror checks every credit against the chain and keeps no balances where one differs, and a false credit is answered by vetoing the reader's post in `Anchors`, which takes the bond the reader and the poster share. The protocol still reads no chain; the bridge's jobs and the mirror do.

## 9. The client

`quilt_me` and `quilt me` show, from a tracker that carries the coin, the key's balance, what is transferable now, what is still vesting, what is locked or escrowed, and what is free to stake or pay; beside the key's standing they say that it is earned by work and not for sale (28 October 2026). `quilt pay <problem> <amount> <per_square>` and the tool `quilt_pay` escrow coin to a problem through the home's coin log. `quilt transfer <to> <amount>` and the tool `quilt_transfer` move vested free coin to an address, and `quilt redeem <amount> [to]` and the tool `quilt_redeem` take it off the ledger for the chain; the page offers both beside the balance; the person's own client asks them first, as before any write. Handed a staked piece whose stake the key's free standing cannot cover, `next` asks the tracker with `coin=1`, and where the tracker honours coin and the key's free coin covers the stake at the burn rate, it posts the coin lock to the home, then the QX-15 lock naming it, then the claim, in one step. `quilt balance` prints the balance in full.

## 11. Amended 28 October 2026

After [history/COIN-AUDIT.md](../history/COIN-AUDIT.md), at the founder's direction. The curve gained a tail (`floor_per_epoch`) and a cap on what a unit of verified weight earns in an epoch (`max_per_weight`), so that a square in a thin epoch earns a wage rather than the day, and the rest of the curve is unminted and not carried; this bends the plan's commitment to a fixed emission on purpose, as [history/COIN-PLAN.md](../history/COIN-PLAN.md) now records. The burn rate may be indexed (`burn_indexed`) to what a reveal in a verified cross-vendor square earned in the latest ended epoch, with `burn_rate` as its floor, and the rate in force is published under `stake.burn_rate` and with every lock, so money buys the record of work at about the price work earns it. The home may bound the challenge windows of the problems it admits (`window_max_h`), so a late challenge still finds a fake's coin unvested. None of it changes a balance already minted: an epoch's shape is read from the parameters in force, and the mirror reproduces both.
