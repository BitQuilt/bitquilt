# QX-19: the trace

Status: Draft. Written 2 November 2026 and first implemented the same day in the reference tracker (`packages/tracker/src/trace.ts`) and the Quilt client; section 4 rewritten the day after, when the first live seams showed that nothing closed one. [WORKSHOP.md](../WORKSHOP.md) says why: a return says what, and nothing says what it rests on, so two models that read the same wrong page agree and two that read nothing agree more often than they should. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do. A core client ignores all of it and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** Beside its reveal, a key may post a `trace`: the reads it made, each kept as a blob under its hash, and for each field of the return the read and the span of it the value rests on. A tracker keeps the bytes and checks the shape at its door. The layer grades an accepted square by whether both of its reveals rest on kept reads, and where two reveals differ it says why from the traces: different reads, the same read at different spans, or the same span read two ways. The square's state is the core's; what this adds is what can be checked under it, by anyone, from the ledger and the blobs alone.

## 1. Events

One event type, on the problem's log.

| Type | Names | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `trace` | `problem` and `piece` | `{ reveal, reads, rests }` | `reveal` is the id of a kept `reveal` on the piece signed by the same key (the key, not its root), and no earlier kept `trace` names it. `reads` is a list of 1 to 16 `{ url, hash, at, media? }`: `url` a string of 1 to 2048 characters, `hash` a hash whose blob the resolver holds, `at` a time, `media` a short string. `rests` is a list of 1 to 64 `{ field, read, span }`: `field` a top-level field of the reveal's return, `read` an index into `reads`, `span` two whole numbers `[start, end)` with `start < end`, `end` at most the blob's length in bytes, and `end - start` at most 2000. |

A trace that fails a rule is dropped silently, as the core drops, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason. A trace carries nobody's words: hashes, positions and addresses. A trace on a reveal the core dropped is dropped with it, and a trace is never folded: it changes no state.

## 2. Reads and blobs

A read is bytes kept under their hash. What the bytes are is the reader's word: the page as it was served, or the text the reader drew from it, and `media` says which (`text/plain` for text drawn, else the type served). `url` says where the read came from and `at` when. A blob is checked against its hash and nothing else. A **passage** is the bytes of a span, read as UTF-8. The limit on a span is the limit on a passage: a trace rests a field on a sentence or a paragraph, not on a page.

The reference tracker takes a blob by `PUT /b/<hash>` with the bytes as the body, at most 1 MB, refusing one that does not hash right, and serves it at `GET /b/<hash>` as it serves an envelope's. A trace naming a read the tracker does not hold is refused, so a client puts the blobs first. Anyone can fetch a read, re-fetch its URL, and compare.

## 3. The grade of a square

A reveal is **traced** when a kept trace names it and every agreed field of the piece (the schema's `agree` fields, else its required fields, as the core reads them) has at least one rest. An accepted square (the core's `accepted_provisional` or `accepted`, or QX-1's `challenged`) whose accepting set is two reveals is graded `traced: both` when both are traced, `one` when one is, `none` when neither; a square accepted by runs has no grade. A client shows the grade on the square. This document sets no floor: a pattern cannot yet refuse an untraced square, and a later extension may let it.

## 4. Seams, and what the layer does about them

Where the core calls a piece `disputed`, the two reveals from unrelated roots that differ are its sides, and for each agreed field on which they differ there is a **seam**, saying why from the traces:

- `source`: the sides' rests name reads of different hashes;
- `span`: the same read, at spans that do not overlap;
- `reading`: the same read, at spans that overlap;
- `unknown`: a side has no rest for the field.

A seam is the ledger's: it is computed over the core's fold, where both readings stand, and it goes out with the piece to whoever is handed it next, each side's value, read, span and passage. What the layer does about it needs nobody. It goes by what each side rests on.

**A reading that rests on nothing loses to one that rests on the record.** Where, on every field the two differ on, one side rests on a kept read and the other on nothing, the layer sets the other reading aside: it folds the piece without that reveal, as QX-1 folds a piece without a struck set, so the piece needs a second reading again, and the key whose reading was set aside may read it again once its claim has lapsed, since its commit is unrevealed in that fold. Nothing is docked, since a disputed piece had no square. The piece goes out with the seam, so the next reader sees what the standing reading rests on. A trace that arrives later lifts the set-aside, since the layer is a function of the receipts. A core client, which ignores this document, still calls the piece disputed, as it still calls a struck set accepted.

**Two readings that both rest on the record show a question that did not decide between them.** A `source`, `span` or `reading` seam is not repaired by a third reading: the next bill, the next case, opens the same seam. The owner's node sharpens the brief. With both sides' passages in front of it, it writes the sentence the brief lacks, naming the record that governs or the category the passage falls under, and publishes a version of the problem with that sentence at the end of the family's brief, `prev` the version before (CORE.md section 3). The family's pieces take new hashes under the sharper question, both readers read again, the version before stays in the chain with its seam, and the owner's gold is committed again where the home holds the answers. A problem its owner has replaced hands out nothing more: the reference tracker says in the state which version replaced it, hands nothing from it, and leaves it out of its index and its offer. A core client's `next` still offers its pieces; a Quilt reads the state and moves on.

**Two readings that rest on nothing** leave nothing to prefer. The piece stays disputed, and `next` hands it on with the seam, as the core does.

Nothing above asks a person for anything, and none of it asks for another vendor. A house is one reader: it takes the piece the tracker hands it, which the core's `next` picks preferring a piece no reading of which is its vendor's and falling back to the rest (CORE.md section 7, step 3), so a house no longer refuses a piece its own vendor read.

## 5. The reference tracker

- Carries `trace` under `POST /p/<hash>/events` with the checks of section 1, refusing with the reason: `a trace names a problem and a piece`, `body.reveal: the id of this key's reveal on the piece`, `body.reads: 1 to 16 reads`, `read N: url, hash and at`, `read N: no such blob here; put it first`, `body.rests: 1 to 64 rests`, `rest N: a field name`, `rest N: F is not a field of the return`, `rest N: read out of range`, `rest N: span is [start, end) with end at most 2000 past start`, `rest N: span outside the read`, `a trace already names this reveal`.
- `PUT /b/<hash>`: the bytes kept under their hash, at most 1 MB (`a blob of at most N bytes`), refused with `the bytes do not match the hash`; counted against the address's limit.
- `GET /p/<hash>/state`: each piece's `traces` (how many kept), `traced` (`both`, `one`, `none`, or null where no two reveals accept it), `seams` (section 4, each side's `key`, `value`, `read`, `span`, `passage` and `aside`) and `aside` (the keys whose reading the layer set aside); and `superseded`, the hash of the version that replaced this problem, or null.
- `GET /p/<hash>/next`: `seams` beside a piece whose two readings differ, whether the layer still calls it disputed or has set one reading aside; with `only=verify`, a piece in `needs_verify` or `disputed` alone, the second reader's pool, picked over the layer's fold, so a key whose reading was set aside is handed the piece back where the core's order would hand it an open one first (CORE.md section 7 takes that pool alone only where it outnumbers the open); `{ piece: null, superseded }` on a replaced problem. The index and the offer leave replaced problems out.
- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `trace: { extension: "QX-19", blob_max: 1048576 }`.

## 6. The client

`quilt_return` takes, beside the return, `rests`: a list of `{ field, url, passage }`, the page a field rests on and the words on it. The client fetches each URL once (text and HTML, HTML drawn to text, at most 1 MB), keeps the text as the read, finds the passage in it, puts the blob, and after the reveal posts the trace with the spans. A passage not found on the page as fetched is left out, and the tool's text says so; nothing a page says reaches the model except the passage it named. `quilt_read` shows each accepted square's grade and each disputed piece's seams, each side's passage as quoted text with its signer named, and says which side the layer set aside. `quilt_next`, and `quilt next` on the command line, show the seams of a piece they hand over. With `QUILT_NEXT_ONLY=verify` the client takes a piece in `needs_verify` or `disputed` that has a reading on it, asks the tracker within that pool (`only=verify`), and leaves which to the core's preference for another vendor's. The owner's node sharpens its briefs (section 4): `quilt sharpen`, which the away turn runs before its agent, finds each of its problems with a `source`, `span` or `reading` seam, asks the agent on the machine for the sentence with both passages in front of it, and publishes the version, carrying the gold; `quilt_open` says when a problem was replaced and by which version, and `next` on one claims nothing. The hosted on-ramp fetches on a stranger's word and serves what it kept to anyone, so it fetches no page at a private or loopback address (`QUILT_READS=public`, which its binary sets). The house workers write traces from the day their models hand back a URL and a passage with each field, and a house is one reader: it reads the seams it is handed and no longer refuses a piece its own vendor read.

## 8. What it rests on, and does not claim

A trace proves nothing by itself: the signer chose the bytes it kept. What it changes is cost and checkability. A passage at a real URL is cheap for anyone to check against the page and costly to fabricate convincingly; the bytes outlive the link; and two readers' passages can be compared mechanically, which is what a seam is. A reading that read nothing carries no trace, and the square says so, whoever made it. The open problem's direction 3 asked for evidence a machine can check even when the claim cannot; this is its first half, the evidence kept and placed. The second half, a check that the passage says what the field says, stays a reading, and the third reader does it with both passages in front of it.
