# QX-12: anchoring a chain's head outside the ledger

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 26 October 2026 in the mirror job (`packages/mirror/src/anchor.ts`). [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [PROTOCOL.md](../PROTOCOL.md), The mirror, names this one; QX-11 gives the chained lines it anchors.

**Abstract.** A resolver's chain (QX-11) proves that nothing in it was changed after the line that followed it was written. It does not prove when any line was written: the resolver stamps the times, and the resolver's keeper could rewrite the whole chain from a point and stamp it again. Anchoring puts each head outside the ledger, in a place no keeper controls. The mirror job stamps the head of every chain it holds through OpenTimestamps, whose calendars aggregate digests into Bitcoin transactions; a block then attests that the head existed by that block's time. Rewriting the past would have to rewrite Bitcoin.

## 1. What is stamped

The **head** of a chain is the `hash` of its last chained receipt line, 32 bytes as hex. Every chain the mirror holds has one: each problem's, and the identity log's. The head is what the mirror submits; nothing of the events themselves leaves the mirror, since the head commits to all of them.

## 2. The stamp

The mirror posts the 32-byte digest to a calendar's `/digest` and receives a timestamp: operations from the digest to a commitment the calendar will carry into a transaction, ending in a pending attestation that names the calendar. The mirror writes:

- `anchors/<problem>/<seq>.ots`: a detached OpenTimestamps file of the digest: the reference client's header, the sha256 file-digest op, the digest, then the calendar's timestamp. The standard tool reads it: `ots upgrade` completes the pending attestation once a block carries it, and `ots verify` checks the file against the head.
- `anchors/<problem>/<seq>.json`: the problem, the sequence number, the head, the calendar, and the time of the stamp as the mirror saw it.

A head already stamped is kept as it is. A stamp is of a moment; the chain's own `prev` links mean a later head's stamp also bounds every earlier line.

## 3. What a reader learns

Holding the mirror, a reader verifies the chain (QX-11) and then the stamps: the head at `seq` was committed to a Bitcoin block at the block's time. A resolver that later serves a different line at or before that `seq` has rewritten its past, and the stamp says so. A line after the last stamped head is bounded only by the next stamp; the mirror job runs every fifteen minutes and stamps what grew, so the bound is at most that long plus the calendar's own delay to a block.

## 4. Calendars

The mirror asks `https://a.pool.opentimestamps.org`, `https://b.pool.opentimestamps.org` and `https://a.pool.eternitywall.com` in that order and keeps the first answer; `--calendars` names others. A calendar is a convenience, not a trust: the attestation it returns is only as good as the block it ends in, which anyone checks.

## 5. What this does not do

- It does not make a chain correct, only old. A line stamped yesterday is a line that existed yesterday.
- It does not complete the proof. The pending attestation becomes a Bitcoin attestation when the calendar's transaction confirms, hours later; `ots upgrade` fetches it, and the mirror job may run it on a later pass.
- It does not anchor a resolver's chain the mirror does not hold.

## 6. Tests

`packages/mirror/test/anchor.test.ts` holds the file layout, the sidecar, the keeping of a stamped head, the passing over of a calendar that fails, and the refusal of a malformed head; `mirror.test.ts` holds that the job anchors each chain's head when asked to.
