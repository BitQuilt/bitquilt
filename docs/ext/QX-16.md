# QX-16: gold pieces

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 26 October 2026 in the reference tracker (`packages/tracker/src/gold.ts`, folded in `challenge.ts`) and the Quilt client; amended twice on 27 October 2026 (section 10). [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [COIN.md](../COIN.md) says what a verified square is for; QX-1 gives the strike this one applies and the challenge that can undo it; QX-15 gives the locks it forfeits and the standing the owner stakes. A core client ignores all of it and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** The core accepts a square when two productions agree, and nothing in the core knows whether they agreed on the truth. Where an owner knows some answers in advance, this extension lets them say so before the work, by hash: a commitment to a piece and the agreed fields of its answer together, with its own nonce, in the core's own commit recipe, backed by a stake of the owner's standing. The commitments are blind: the ledger shows how many there are and not which pieces they are on, so every square is a possible audit until the owner reveals, naming the piece. A set that agrees with the gold makes the square **verified**, which is the one thing the coin's emission counts ([COIN.md](../COIN.md)). A set that differs is a **miss**: struck without a jury, as a held challenge strikes, its roots docked and their locks forfeited. The owner can lie with gold, so a miss is itself challengeable by a struck root under QX-1, and a challenge that holds restores the set and forfeits the owner's stake.

## 1. Events

Two event types, both on the problem's log.

| Type | Names | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gold` | `problem` | `{ commitments: [h, …], stake }` | The signer's root is the problem's owner's root at the receipt. `commitments` is a list of hashes, at least one, each once, none kept by an earlier gold of the problem; each `h` is `commitHash({ piece, projection }, nonce)` for a piece of the problem whose return has agreed fields (a schema; not `check` or `score`), and the ledger cannot tell which. `stake` is a whole number at least the number of commitments times the problem's **share**: the largest, over the pieces whose return has agreed fields, of the piece's own share, which is, for each root a strike of the piece's accepting set would dock (two for a piece whose set is two reveals, an `agree` piece and the `human` and `judge` kinds, one for an `attest` piece), the piece's stake under the QX-15 rule in force, or 1 where none is asked. Where no stake is asked and the pieces are `agree`, the share is 2, what a miss takes from the two roots it docks. The owner's free standing (QX-15 section 3) at the receipt covers `stake`. |
| `gold_reveal` | `problem` and `piece` | `{ projection, nonce }` | The signer's root is the owner's. No commitment has been revealed on the piece. `commitHash({ piece, projection }, nonce)` equals a pending commitment of a gold of the problem not forfeited. The piece's return has agreed fields, and the piece had no production (a claim, commit, reveal or run) received before that gold. The piece has an accepting set completed at or before the receipt, whose window has not closed. `projection` is an object. |

A delegate signs either type only with it in `scope`. An event that fails a rule is dropped silently, as the core drops, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason.

## 2. The projection and the commitment

The **agreed fields** of a piece are what the core compares two returns on: the piece's `agree` list, else its schema's `required`. The owner's **projection** of an answer is an object holding those fields of the answer and no other, in canonical JSON. The commitment is `commitHash({ piece, projection }, nonce)`: the core's recipe for a `commit`, SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of an object holding the piece's hash under `piece` and the projection under `projection`, followed by the nonce. Nothing about the answer, and nothing about the piece, leaves the owner's machine before the reveal: a commitment made with a fresh nonce says nothing about either to anyone holding the ledger, and the client lists a gold's commitments in hash order, so the order says nothing. A reader learns how many answers the owner committed to, and no more.

An owner may post more than one gold on a problem. A commitment never revealed lapses (section 3), so a commitment made as a decoy, to keep the count from saying which pieces are plain, costs the owner its share of stake until the problem settles, and nothing else.

## 3. The decision

At a kept reveal, each reveal of the piece's accepting set is compared to the projection on the agreed fields as the core compares two returns for agreement.

- **Match**: every reveal of the set agrees with the projection. The square is **verified**. The set's locks are released at the window's close as usual.
- **Miss**: a reveal of the set differs. The set is **struck** as a held challenge strikes it (QX-1 section 6): its events are left out of the fold from the reveal's receipt, each root of the set takes QX-1's minus one and its point for the set is withdrawn, and each root's lock on the piece is forfeited (QX-15 section 3). The piece is what the core's reducer computes without those events, and is offered again. A miss opens a window of the problem's `challenge_window_h` from the reveal's receipt.
- A piece on which nothing has been revealed is neither verified nor missed; it is what the core says. A commitment still pending once the problem has **settled**, complete with every window closed, has **lapsed**: the owner may not reveal it, and whatever square it was on is accepted and not verified. A pending commitment of a gold forfeited under section 4 is **dropped**.

## 4. The challenge on a miss

Inside the window a miss opens, a root of the struck set may post a QX-1 `challenge` on the piece. It is a challenge against the gold, not against an acceptance: the parties are the problem's owner; the pool is QX-1's with the struck roots left out as well; the stake may be 0, since the miss may have left the challenger nothing to stake (a new root's one point, its point for the set and its lock all go with the strike), and a stake above 0 is against the challenger's free standing as QX-1 has it; the seats, the lapses and the majority are QX-1's. `for: challenger` says the gold was wrong; `for: author` says the set was.

- **Held**: the strike is undone from the decision: the set's events return to the fold, each struck root regains from the decision the point docked, its point for the set and its lock, and the challenger's standing rises by the stake. The gold's whole stake is forfeited from the owner's standing from the decision, and the gold's commitments not yet revealed are dropped. The piece's gold is **overruled**: the square is accepted as the core had it and not verified.
- **Failed**: the miss stands; the challenger's standing falls by the stake.
- **Void**: nothing is won or lost; the miss stands.

QX-1's rules that one challenge is open on a piece at a time and that a piece is challenged once hold here too.

## 5. The owner's stake

A gold's `stake` is locked standing of the owner from the gold's receipt (counted in QX-15's **locked**, so the owner does not stake it twice) until every commitment of it is final: matched and the piece's window closed; missed and the miss's window closed with no challenge, or its challenge decided; lapsed, once the problem has settled; or dropped. A held challenge forfeits it whole, from the decision. A commitment on a piece that is never accepted keeps the stake live until the problem settles; an owner commits only to what they want done.

## 6. States

A client with this extension holds, beside the core's states, each piece's `gold`, one of `null` (nothing revealed on it), `match`, `miss` and `overruled`, and `verified`, true where the gold is `match`; and for the problem a count of its commitments, `committed`, `pending`, `verified`, `missed`, `overruled` and `lapsed`, which is all the ledger says of the commitments not yet revealed. Where this layer strikes a set the core still says accepted within its window, as EXTENSIONS.md allows; where the layer says verified the core says accepted. A struck piece is offered again by `next` run on this layer's fold, as after a held challenge.

## 7. The reference tracker

- Carries `gold` and `gold_reveal` under `POST /p/<hash>/events` with the checks of section 1, refusing with the reason: `the owner alone commits gold`, `body.commitments: a list of commitment hashes, at least one`, `body.commitments: each once`, `a commitment is kept already`, `gold applies to a piece whose return has agreed fields` (a problem with no such piece), `the gold asks a stake of at least N`, `free standing M is below the gold stake of N`, `the owner alone reveals gold`, `the gold on this piece is revealed`, `the reveal matches no commitment kept on this problem`, `gold names a piece already worked: <name>`, `nothing accepted on this piece yet`, `the window has closed`.
- A challenge on a miss is admitted under section 4 with a stake of 0 or more, and refused, outside its window, with `outside the window`, and from a root not struck with `only a root of the struck set challenges a miss`. A stake of 0 against an acceptance is QX-1's refusal, `a challenge on an acceptance stakes at least 1`.
- `GET /p/<hash>/state` carries each piece's `gold` and `verified`, and the problem's `gold` count.
- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `gold: { extension: "QX-16" }`.

## 8. The client

A pattern may carry `gold: { answers: { [piece name]: answer }, stake? }`. `publish` projects each answer to the piece's agreed fields, draws a nonce for each, commits each answer bound to its piece, posts the `gold` right after the envelope with the commitments in hash order and the stake asked (the share times the count when none is given), and keeps the projections and nonces in the library by piece, which never leaves the home. `attention` lists each committed piece whose acceptance stands and on which nothing is revealed, as a reveal due; `quilt gold reveal <problem> <piece>` and the tool `quilt_reveal_gold` post the reveal from the library, naming the piece on the ledger for the first time, and the person's own client asks them first. `quilt gold commit <problem> <answers.json>` posts a gold after publishing, refusing a piece somebody has worked since the reveal on it would be refused, with the stake the shares come to: an owner whose standing is short at publishing commits the gold once it is not. `quilt open` and `quilt_read` say how many answers the owner committed without saying which, and count the verified and missed squares, naming a verified square as verified beside its accepted returns. `quilt challenge <problem> <piece> 0 <ground>` and the tool `quilt_challenge` with a stake of 0 post a struck root's challenge on a miss.

## 9. Tests

`packages/tracker/test/gold.test.ts` holds the rules: a gold from the owner kept and one from anyone else refused; a commitment list that is not hashes, or repeats one, or repeats a kept one refused; a stake below the share times the count (two a commitment under a rule of 1, and two a commitment where no stake is asked) and a stake over free standing refused; the owner's stake counted as locked; the ledger counting the commitments and no gold event naming a piece, and no piece showing gold before its reveal; a reveal refused on a piece worked before the gold, before acceptance, after the window, twice, and when it matches no commitment; a match marking the square verified; a miss striking the set, docking its roots, forfeiting their locks and sending the piece back; a challenge on the miss from a struck root, staking nothing, that holds, restoring the set, forfeiting the owner's stake and dropping the rest of the gold; a challenge from a root not struck refused, and a stake of 0 against an acceptance refused; a commitment lapsing once the problem has settled; a new root the miss left at zero standing challenging it for nothing, and the hold restoring its two points. `packages/client/test/gold.test.ts` holds the client: `publish` with gold naming no piece on the ledger, the library keeping the nonces, attention listing a reveal due, `quilt_reveal_gold` posting it, the summary counting the commitments and the verified squares, a commit on a worked piece refused, a stake of 0 on a miss passing the client and the door, and the tools' words describing and never instructing.

## 10. Changes

Amended 27 October 2026, after a review of griefing. A problem's owner cannot challenge on their own problem (QX-1 section 1), so gold was the one way an owner could strike honest sets without a jury, and the remedy section 4 gives a struck root asked a stake of at least 1. A new root begins at 1, earns 1 for the set, and loses both with the strike (the set's point withdrawn, one docked), so under the rule as first written the newest workers were the ones who could not answer a wrong gold. Two things changed. A struck root's challenge on a miss may stake 0. And a piece's share of the owner's stake counts each root a miss would dock, two for an `agree` piece where it was one, so the owner stakes what the workers stand to lose.

Amended again the same day, at the founder's direction. As first written a gold's commitments were keyed by piece hash, so the ledger showed which pieces carried gold, and a worker after the coin's emission could take those pieces and leave the rest: `next` hands the rarest piece, but a claim may name any piece and a decline sends one back. Gold is now blind. A commitment binds the piece and the answer under one hash, the ledger counts a problem's commitments and names no piece until the owner's reveal does, the stake covers each commitment at the largest share among the pieces a gold can be on, since the ledger cannot tell which piece a commitment is for, and a commitment lapses when the problem settles rather than when a piece's window closes. Every square is a possible audit, so the honest way to work a problem with gold on it is to work every piece the same way. The tracker, the client and their tests changed with the document; the core is untouched.
