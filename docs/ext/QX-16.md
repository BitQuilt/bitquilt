# QX-16: gold pieces

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 26 October 2026 in the reference tracker (`packages/tracker/src/gold.ts`, folded in `challenge.ts`) and the Quilt client. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [COIN.md](../COIN.md) says what a verified square is for; QX-1 gives the strike this one applies and the challenge that can undo it; QX-15 gives the locks it forfeits and the standing the owner stakes. A core client ignores all of it and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** The core accepts a square when two productions agree, and nothing in the core knows whether they agreed on the truth. Where an owner knows some answers in advance, this extension lets them say so before the work, by hash: a commitment to the agreed fields of each answer, with its own nonce, in the core's own commit recipe, backed by a stake of the owner's standing. After a piece is accepted the owner reveals. A set that agrees with the gold makes the square **verified**, which is the one thing the coin's emission counts ([COIN.md](../COIN.md)). A set that differs is a **miss**: struck without a jury, as a held challenge strikes, its roots docked and their locks forfeited. The owner can lie with gold, so a miss is itself challengeable by a struck root under QX-1, and a challenge that holds restores the set and forfeits the owner's stake.

## 1. Events

Two event types, both on the problem's log.

| Type | Names | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gold` | `problem` | `{ commitments: { [piece hash]: h }, stake }` | The signer's root is the problem's owner's root at the receipt. Each key is the hash of a piece of the problem whose return has agreed fields (a schema; not `check` or `score`), and each `h` is a hash. A commitment is kept only for a piece with no production on it before the receipt and no commitment kept already; the reference tracker refuses the whole event at its door naming the piece, and a reader of a ledger another door admitted drops the one and keeps the rest. `stake` is a whole number at least the sum, over the commitments kept, of each piece's **share**: its stake under the QX-15 rule in force, or 1 where none is asked. The owner's free standing (QX-15 section 3) at the receipt covers `stake`. |
| `gold_reveal` | `problem` and `piece` | `{ projection, nonce }` | The signer's root is the owner's. The piece has a kept commitment, not yet revealed, and `commitHash(projection, nonce)` equals it. The piece has an accepting set completed at or before the receipt, whose window has not closed. `projection` is an object. |

A delegate signs either type only with it in `scope`. An event that fails a rule is dropped silently, as the core drops, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason.

## 2. The projection and the commitment

The **agreed fields** of a piece are what the core compares two returns on: the piece's `agree` list, else its schema's `required`. The owner's **projection** of an answer is an object holding those fields of the answer and no other, in canonical JSON. The commitment is `commitHash(projection, nonce)`, the core's recipe for a `commit`: SHA-256 over the canonical JSON followed by the nonce. Nothing about the answer leaves the owner's machine before the reveal; a commitment made with a fresh nonce says nothing about the answer to anyone holding the ledger.

## 3. The decision

At a kept reveal, each reveal of the piece's accepting set is compared to the projection on the agreed fields as the core compares two returns for agreement.

- **Match**: every reveal of the set agrees with the projection. The square is **verified**. The set's locks are released at the window's close as usual.
- **Miss**: a reveal of the set differs. The set is **struck** as a held challenge strikes it (QX-1 section 6): its events are left out of the fold from the reveal's receipt, each root of the set takes QX-1's minus one and its point for the set is withdrawn, and each root's lock on the piece is forfeited (QX-15 section 3). The piece is what the core's reducer computes without those events, and is offered again. A miss opens a window of the problem's `challenge_window_h` from the reveal's receipt.
- A piece with no kept commitment is neither verified nor missed; it is what the core says. A kept commitment whose piece reaches `accepted` with no reveal has **lapsed**: the owner may not reveal it, and the square is accepted and not verified.

## 4. The challenge on a miss

Inside the window a miss opens, a root of the struck set may post a QX-1 `challenge` on the piece. It is a challenge against the gold, not against an acceptance: the parties are the problem's owner; the pool is QX-1's with the struck roots left out as well; the stake is against the challenger's free standing as always; the seats, the lapses and the majority are QX-1's. `for: challenger` says the gold was wrong; `for: author` says the set was.

- **Held**: the strike is undone from the decision: the set's events return to the fold, each struck root regains from the decision the point docked, its point for the set and its lock, and the challenger's standing rises by the stake. The gold's whole stake is forfeited from the owner's standing from the decision, and the gold's commitments not yet revealed are dropped. The piece's gold is **overruled**: the square is accepted as the core had it and not verified.
- **Failed**: the miss stands; the challenger's standing falls by the stake.
- **Void**: nothing is won or lost; the miss stands.

QX-1's rules that one challenge is open on a piece at a time and that a piece is challenged once hold here too.

## 5. The owner's stake

A gold's `stake` is locked standing of the owner from the gold's receipt (counted in QX-15's **locked**, so the owner does not stake it twice) until every kept commitment is final: matched and the piece's window closed; missed and the miss's window closed with no challenge, or its challenge decided; or lapsed. A held challenge forfeits it whole, from the decision. A commitment on a piece that is never accepted keeps the stake live; an owner commits only to what they want done.

## 6. States

A client with this extension holds, beside the core's states, each piece's `gold`, one of `null` (no commitment kept), `pending`, `match`, `miss`, `overruled` and `lapsed`, and `verified`, true where the gold is `match`. Where this layer strikes a set the core still says accepted within its window, as EXTENSIONS.md allows; where the layer says verified the core says accepted. A struck piece is offered again by `next` run on this layer's fold, as after a held challenge.

## 7. The reference tracker

- Carries `gold` and `gold_reveal` under `POST /p/<hash>/events` with the checks of section 1, refusing with the reason: `the owner alone commits gold`, `gold names a piece this problem does not have`, `gold applies to a piece whose return has agreed fields`, `gold names a piece already worked: <name>`, `this piece has its gold`, `the gold asks a stake of at least N`, `free standing M is below the gold stake of N`, `the owner alone reveals gold`, `no gold on this piece`, `the gold on this piece is revealed`, `nothing accepted on this piece yet`, `the window has closed`, `the reveal does not match the commitment`.
- A challenge on a miss is admitted under section 4 and refused, outside its window, with `outside the window`, and from a root not struck with `only a root of the struck set challenges a miss`.
- `GET /p/<hash>/state` carries each piece's `gold` and `verified`.
- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `gold: { extension: "QX-16" }`.

## 8. The client

A pattern may carry `gold: { answers: { [piece name]: answer }, stake? }`. `publish` projects each answer to the piece's agreed fields, draws a nonce for each, posts the `gold` right after the envelope with the stake asked (the sum of the shares when none is given), and keeps the projections and nonces in the library, which never leaves the home. `attention` lists each committed piece whose acceptance stands and whose gold is pending, as a reveal due; `quilt gold reveal <problem> <piece>` and the tool `quilt_reveal_gold` post the reveal from the library, and the person's own client asks them first. `quilt gold commit <problem> <answers.json>` posts a gold after publishing, on pieces nobody has worked yet, with the stake the shares come to: an owner whose standing is short at publishing commits the gold once it is not. `quilt open` and `quilt_read` count the committed, verified and missed squares, and name a verified square as verified beside its accepted returns.

## 9. Tests

`packages/tracker/test/gold.test.ts` holds the rules: a gold from the owner kept and one from anyone else refused; a stake below the shares, a stake over free standing, a second commitment on a piece and a commitment on a piece already worked refused; the owner's stake counted as locked; a reveal refused before acceptance, after the window, twice, and when it does not match; a match marking the square verified; a miss striking the set, docking its roots, forfeiting their locks and sending the piece back; a challenge on the miss from a struck root that holds, restoring the set, forfeiting the owner's stake and dropping the rest of the gold; a challenge from a root not struck refused. `packages/client/test/gold.test.ts` holds the client: `publish` with gold, the library keeping the nonces, attention listing a reveal due, `quilt_reveal_gold` posting it, the summary counting verified squares, and the tools' words describing and never instructing.
