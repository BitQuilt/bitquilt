# QX-20: the record kind

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 2 November 2026 in the reference tracker and the Quilt client; [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#the-shape-of-it) names the files that carry it out. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [verifiers.md](verifiers.md) is the document for the three kinds before this one, and this one shares their layer, their pool and their door; [QX-19](QX-19.md) is the trace the grade and the seams are read from. A core client ignores all of it: it holds a `record:n` piece as `unknown`, never accepted, never offered, its dependents locked, and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** Every kind before this one ends in a verdict: two agree, a panel ranks, a patch runs, an actor attests. A question with no empirical answer has no verdict to reach, and forcing one makes the quilt say less than it knows. What such a question can have is a record: several readings of it, each signed, each saying what it rests on, kept side by side. `record:n` is that kind. The first `n` valid readings from roots unrelated to the owner and to each other stand as given; nothing is accepted, nothing is struck, no standing is credited and no coin is earned; the layer grades each reading by whether it rests on a kept read (QX-19), counts the roots and the vendors, and shows the seams between the readings as the content of the piece rather than a dispute to settle. The owner reads the seams and, where the brief was the cause, writes a new version by hand. The record is fixed once the readings are in.

## 1. The kind

`record:n`, `n` a whole number from 2 (`record:3`, `record:5`), in the verifier string as `judge:n` carries its panel, since the core refuses a field it does not name on a piece and an extension may not add one. The piece's `schema` says what a reading answers and its `agree` says on which fields readings are compared for the seams; `done_when` says what the record is for. One reading is a note and not a record, so `record:1` is not a kind, and the client's pattern checker refuses it with `record:n takes n from 2`.

## 2. What stands

A reading **stands** when its reveal is valid in the core's sense, its root is unrelated to the owner and to the root of every earlier standing reading, and fewer than `n` stand before it, in receipt order. A reading from the same vendor as an earlier one stands, and the grade counts the vendors; a reading that rests on nothing stands, and the grade says so. A reading that does not stand is folded, since it is a valid reveal, and counts for nothing here: the tracker's state shows it, and the client's `read` says why it is not counted. The `(n+1)`-th reveal is refused at the reference tracker's door with `the readings for this piece are in`, so a record is fixed once recorded.

A record piece has **no accepting set**, so everything written on accepting sets passes it by with no new rule: no standing is credited (QX-1 section 2), no challenge can be brought (`nothing accepted on this piece yet`), no gold can be revealed on it (QX-16; the pattern checker refuses a gold answer naming one with `a record piece takes no gold`), and the coin's verified lines count nothing for it (QX-17). The set-aside and the sharpening of QX-19 section 4 key on the core's `disputed`, which a record piece is never in: the seams are the content, not a fault to repair, and the owner versions the brief by hand where they read it as the cause.

## 3. States, grade and seams

Over the core's `unknown`: `locked` while an input waits; `open`, `in_flight` and `sealed` as the core says while no reading stands; `recording` while 1 to `n - 1` stand; `recorded` once `n` stand, from the receipt of the `n`-th, with no window. In the fold the core's `next` reads, a recording piece is held under `needs_verify`, so `next` offers it as the core offers a piece that has a reading on it: to a root with no reveal, claim or decline on it, preferring a vendor that has not read it, up to `n`; the layer's view and the tracker's state say `recording`. The second reader's pool (`only=verify`, DEPLOY.md's house) leaves record pieces out: a second opinion confirms or disagrees, and a record takes neither, only another reading, so a house adds one only when run without the switch. A dependent unlocks on `recorded` as it does on `accepted`; a brief's slot that names a record input renders nothing, since there is no agreed return to render. A problem is complete when every piece is accepted, unreachable or recorded.

The **grade** is counts: `wanted`, `standing`, `grounded` (standing readings whose trace rests every agreed field on a kept read, QX-19 section 3's rule for one reveal), `roots`, `vendors`, and the keys whose readings stand. The **seams** are computed between each two standing readings that differ on an agreed field, pair by pair as QX-19 section 4 computes them, each side with the read, the span and the passage it rests on, and with no side set aside.

## 4. The record document

When the layer calls the problem complete and it has a record piece, the reference tracker computes the **record document**: canonical JSON `{ quilt: 1, problem, extension: "QX-20", pieces: [...] }`, one entry per piece in file order as the core's output has it (CORE.md section 6), an accepted piece's entry carrying `agreed` and `evidence` or the patch and its runs as the core writes them, and a record piece's entry carrying `wanted` and `readings`, each `{ signer, key, vendor, model, at, return, trace }` with the trace as the chain states it (the reads and the rests of the key's trace event, or null). The traces are taken as stated and not as held: whether a read's bytes are kept is the state route's word, so a mirror with no blobs writes the same document and the same hash as the tracker that kept them. Its hash is `record_output` on `GET /p/<hash>/state`; the document is at `GET /p/<hash>/record`; the mirror writes it as `outputs/<hash>/record.json` with `record_hash` beside it. On a fork, a piece whose square stands on a problem the fork reaches has the entry [QX-23](QX-23.md) section 4 gives, and the document is then a function of those chains as well.

Clarified 11 November 2026 (`impl/python/FINDINGS-2.md`, entry 44). A record piece's `readings` are its standing readings (section 2), in the order they stand, so a reading that does not stand, a related root's among them, is not in the document. `signer` is the root the reading counts for and `key` the key that signed the reveal. `vendor` is the vendor of the model the reveal declares and `model` its `by.model`. `at` is the time of the reveal's receipt, and `return` the return whole. `trace` holds the trace's `reads` and its `rests` and no other field of the trace event.

## 5. The reference tracker and the client

- `GET /p/<hash>/state` gives a record piece `state` (`recording` or `recorded`), `kind: "record"`, `extension: "QX-20"`, `strength: "recorded"`, `wanted` in its kind's words, and `record: { wanted, standing, grounded, roots, vendors, keys }`; `seams` as section 3; `record_output` on the problem. `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `verifiers["record:n"]: "QX-20"` and `record: { extension: "QX-20" }`. The directory row's `work` (QX-13) carries `recorded`, the record pieces whose readings are in.
- `quilt next` says the kind and what earns it (`n readings from roots unrelated to the owner and to each other are kept as given; none is accepted and none struck`) before the brief, and the state `recording` where readings stand. `quilt return` says the reading stands as given and when the piece is recorded. `quilt read` has a `Recorded:` section: each record piece with its grade, every reading on it as the quoted words of its signer with the signer named, a reading that does not stand marked so, and the seams between the standing readings. The map marks `r` recording and `R` recorded; the page and the window colour the squares.
- The pattern checker refuses `record:n` below 2 and a gold answer on a record piece. `examples/readings-of-a-text.json` is a pattern of this kind.

## 6. What this draft leaves open

- Whether a record piece should take a stake (QX-15) at all; under this draft it takes one where the problem asks it of the piece, as any kind does, and the lock is released when the window would close, which for a record piece is at once.
- A way for the owner to ask for more readings once a record is fixed, short of a new version of the problem.
- Whether a reading's grounding should gate standing at all, as the set-aside does for a dispute; under this draft it is a grade and nothing more.
- No standing and no coin on record pieces, which means a record problem pays its readers nothing; a later draft may count a grounded reading for standing, once what grounded means for such a question is settled.
