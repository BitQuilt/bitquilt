# QX-15: stake at the door

Status: Draft. Written and first implemented 26 October 2026 in the reference tracker (`packages/tracker/src/stake.ts`, folded in `challenge.ts`) and the Quilt client, with standing as the one thing a root can lock; the coin lock this document names is refused by this version and is the work of QX-17. [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) says what an extension may do; [COIN.md](../COIN.md) says why the protocol admits a stake and [COIN-PLAN.md](../COIN-PLAN.md) the order it is built in; QX-1 gives the standing this one locks and the strike that forfeits it. A core client ignores all of it and computes the same map it did before.

**Abstract.** The core offers a piece to any key that can do it, and a wrong square costs the key that made it nothing but the standing QX-1 docks when a challenge holds. This extension lets a problem's owner ask that a claim on its pieces be backed by a stake. A root backs its work on a piece by locking standing, or coin, before it claims; a tracker admits a claim, a commit, a reveal or a run on a staked piece only from a root with a live lock on it. A lock is released when the root's work on the piece is over without a strike, and forfeited when an accepting set the root was in is struck. Standing earned by work satisfies any stake, and a problem that asks none is open to a key with nothing, which is what [EXTENSIONS.md](../EXTENSIONS.md) now requires of every extension.

## 1. Events

Two event types, both on the problem's log.

| Type | Names | Body | Valid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `stake_rule` | `problem` | `{ min, pieces? }` | `min` is a whole number from 0. `pieces`, if present, is a list of family or piece names the problem has; absent, the rule covers every piece. The signer's root is the problem's owner's root at the receipt. A later rule from the owner replaces the earlier one from its receipt. |
| `lock` | `problem` and `piece` | `{ kind: "standing", amount }`, or `{ kind: "coin", amount, id }` | The rule in force at the receipt asks a stake of the piece, and `amount`, a whole number of at least 1, is at least it. No lock by the signer's root on the piece is live at the receipt. For `standing`: the root's free standing (section 3) at the receipt covers `amount`. For `coin`: `id` names a lock on the home ledger's coin log (QX-17) that is the root's, at least `amount`, for this problem and piece, and unspent; a tracker that carries no coin and reads no home refuses it. |

A delegate signs either type only with it in `scope`. An event that fails a rule is dropped silently, as the core drops, and the reference tracker refuses it at the door with the reason.

The rule in force at a time is the latest valid `stake_rule` received at or before it. A piece's **stake** at a time is the rule's `min` when the rule covers the piece and 0 otherwise; a problem with no rule asks nothing of anyone.

## 2. What a lock backs

A root's **production** on a piece is its claims, commits, reveals and runs there. On a piece whose stake is above 0, a tracker admits a production event only from a root with a live lock on the piece at the receipt; the layer, reading a ledger some other door admitted, counts the production as the core does and shows the piece as unbacked. One live lock per root per piece backs everything that root does on it until the lock ends. A lock made under an earlier rule backs the production after the rule rises.

## 3. Standing, locked and free

Beside standing (QX-1 section 2), the layer computes per root and time:

- **locked**: the sum of the amounts of the root's live standing locks;
- **free**: standing less locked, never below zero.

Free standing is what a root may stake on a challenge (QX-1) and lock on a piece. Where QX-1 says a challenge's stake is at most the signer's standing, read free standing: a root that has locked what it has cannot stake it twice.

A lock is **live** from its receipt until it is released or forfeited:

- **Released** at the lock's receipt plus the piece's lease (the piece's `lease_h`, else the problem's) when the root has made no production event on the piece by then.
- Otherwise released when the root's work on the piece is over without a strike: when the piece's accepting set has completed and its window has closed (the core says `accepted`), whether or not the root is in the set; when the root's claim ran out with no commit by it; or when the root's commit was abandoned unrevealed.
- **Forfeited** at the decision of a held challenge (QX-1 section 6) on an accepting set the root is in, or at a gold miss (QX-16) on one. Every root of the set loses its lock: the reveals and, on a `check` or `score` piece, the runs.

A forfeited standing lock is a move of minus its amount on the root's standing from the strike, beside QX-1's own minus one for a struck set. A forfeited coin lock is forfeited on the coin log (QX-17). Whatever the kind, a strike costs at least the stake the piece asked. A lock on a piece that stays `disputed` is live until the piece resolves; the root may not lock on it again until then.

## 4. States

A client with this extension holds, beside the core's states, each piece's stake at `now` and each root's locks. It shows a staked piece with the stake it asks and a square that was produced without a lock as unbacked. Nothing here changes what the core calls the piece: where the layer says a square is backed or not, the core says what it said.

## 5. The reference tracker

- Carries `stake_rule` and `lock` under `POST /p/<hash>/events` with the checks of section 1, and holds `claim`, `commit`, `reveal` and `run` on a staked piece to section 2, refusing with the reason: `the owner alone sets a stake rule`, `names a piece or family this problem does not have`, `this piece asks no stake`, `this piece asks for a stake of N`, `a lock already stands on this piece`, `free standing M is below the lock of N`, `this tracker honours no coin lock`, `the key may not sign a lock`, `this piece asks for a stake of N; lock first`.
- A challenge over free standing is refused with `free standing M is below the stake of N` where a lock is live, and with QX-1's `standing M is below the stake of N` where none is.
- `GET /p/<hash>/state` carries the rule in force as `stake_rule: { min, pieces } | null` and each piece's `stake`.
- `GET /p/<hash>/next` and `GET /offer` leave out a staked piece for a key whose free standing cannot cover it and that holds no lock on it; `/next` names the pieces it left out for that reason as `unaffordable`, and answers `stake` beside the piece it hands over.
- `GET /k/<address>/standing`: the key's root, its standing, locked and free at `now`, and its live locks, each with the problem, the piece, the amount and the receipt.
- `GET /.well-known/quilt` says `stake: { extension: "QX-15", coin: null }`; a tracker that carries the coin (QX-17) or reads a home that does says so in `coin`.

## 6. The client

A pattern may carry `stake: { min, pieces? }`, and `publish` posts the rule right after the envelope; `quilt stake <problem> <min> [<family> ...]` and the owner's `stakeRule` post a later one. Handed a staked piece, `next` posts the `lock` and then the `claim` in one step, and the person's own client asks them once for the pair; the text names the stake and that it is locked until the window closes. `quilt me` and `quilt_me` show standing, locked and free from the first tracker that answers. A hosted root locks standing like any other.

## 7. Tests

`packages/tracker/test/stake.test.ts` holds the rules: a rule from the owner kept and one from anyone else refused; a claim refused without a lock and admitted with one; a lock refused over free standing, below the stake, on an unstaked piece, and twice on one piece; free standing falling and rising; a lock released when the window closes and when nothing was produced in the lease; a lock forfeited by a held challenge; the offers leaving out what a key cannot cover; a coin lock refused by a tracker with no coin. `packages/client/test/stake.test.ts` holds the client: `publish` with a stake, `next` locking then claiming, the text of `quilt_me` and of the piece, and the tools' words describing and never instructing.
