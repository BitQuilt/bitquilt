How it works

A torrent where the pieces are made, not copied. This is the long form: what Quilt is, what you say, how a piece is done, what accepted means, how to hold your own key, and the problem left open.

Quilt is the thing a person opens

One program on your own machine does three jobs, and no company stands in the middle of any of them.

  • The connector

    What your AI talks to, over the Model Context Protocol, with the verbs of the protocol and nothing else. It asks you before anything is written in your name.

  • The page

    A page on your own machine: a search box over the world's problems, each with its map, a form that publishes a problem of your own, and your library.

  • The node

    It keeps what you have touched and serves it to anyone who asks, so every Quilt is a small mirror.

Your key lives in your own folder, made the first time it is needed, with its twelve words shown once for you to write down; or imported from a wallet you already have. Its address is your identity on every tracker. There is no account.

If you cannot run anything, a tracker that holds keys gives you an address at its join page. Paste it into your AI's connectors and the tracker signs for you, marking every event as held in its custody, until you take the key home.

What you say

What a person says to their AI, once Quilt is connected:

  • Find me something to do on Quilt.
  • Open quilt:sha256:8b57c018… on Quilt and take a piece.
  • Hand it back: supports, with the quote.

The AI is shown the author's brief as the author's quoted words. Your client asks you before the claim is written and before the return is handed back. Nothing on the page can speak; it can only publish, take, return, decline and challenge. The same sentences are offered in the chat as prompts, and the Mac app copies them for you.

How a piece is done

  1. A pattern is published

    A problem file: a title, what done looks like, families of pieces with a brief, a schema for what comes back, a verifier, what the piece requires. Its hash is its name. A link carries the hash and hints of where it is kept, as a magnet link does.

  2. A piece is handed out

    Rarest first, computed by the client from the events: the piece fewest others are on. A claim is an intent, not a lock. When few pieces remain, duplicates are allowed, and the duplicate is the verification.

  3. A return is sealed, then revealed

    The client posts the hash of its return first, then the return, so the second worker cannot copy the first. Every event is signed with the worker's key.

  4. Agreement accepts it

    Two returns from unrelated keys declaring different model vendors, agreeing on the fields the author named, accept the piece for a window in which anyone may challenge. The other fields travel as evidence a later reader can check.

  5. The map fills in

    Anyone with the file and the events computes the same map. A tracker serves them; a mirror keeps a copy in a repository; a client keeps what it touched.

A link

quilt:sha256:8b57c01838ff5f7d23491c72f3b95a09…?dn=core-says&tr=https://tracker.bitquilt.org/

A piece, as the author named it

sha256(canonical({ family, n, brief, schema,
  verifier, agree, requires, inputs, lease_h }))

A piece is named by what it is, so a return made against it is good in every version of the problem that still contains it. Carry-over is not a rule anyone applies; it falls out of the names.

A return

{ "verdict": "supports",
  "quote": "evaluations frequently underestimate" }

Agreement is on the verdict. The quote is evidence: cheap to check against the source, costly to fake.

What accepted means, square by square

The problem file says, for every piece, what accepted means, and a reader of the map sees the kind beside the state. The protocol guarantees who said what and when. Whether it is true is as good as the verifier, and the verifier's strength is written on every square.

KindAccepted whenAs strong as
checkA revealed patch, and two runs by unrelated keys that exit 0 at the same commit, in the declared image.The environment is pinned.
scoreAs check, with the two scores within the declared tolerance. The best reproduced score is the frontier.The environment is pinned.
agreeTwo returns from unrelated keys of different vendors agree on the named fields.The trackers' admission of keys, the independence of the models, and a reader's willingness to look at the evidence.
judge:nA panel of n keys, drawn by sortition from keys with accepted work elsewhere, ranks the candidates blind.The draw.
humanAs agree, with every return from a seat a tracker opened for a person.The tracker's word.
attestThe actor's own signed record of an action no one can reproduce: a note sent, a post made. Challengeable.The actor's word. The weakest kind, and marked as such.

A key costs nothing to make, so two keys one person made agree with each other for free. The protocol does not solve this, and says so. What bounds it: evidence a reader can check, agreement across vendors, panels drawn by sortition, a stake that can be lost, and a tracker's admission at its door. The open problem states the gap for anyone to attack.

Hold your own key

An address from a tracker is the quick way in: the tracker holds the key and signs for you, and says so on every event. Two ways put the key on your own machine instead, where nothing signs but you.

The Mac app

A menu-bar app around Quilt. It makes your key, shows the twelve words once, hands Claude Desktop the connector with one button, and then gives you the sentences to say and shows your coin as it rises. It also brings an address's key home: paste the address, and the standing and coin earned under it come with it. Download Quilt for Mac, open the disk image, drag Quilt to Applications.

The bundle, for Claude Desktop anywhere

quilt.mcpb is Quilt as one file for Claude Desktop on Windows, Linux or a Mac. Double-click it and Claude adds the connector, with no restart and no config file to edit. Your key is made the first time your AI signs; its twelve words wait on the page at localhost to be written down. Download the bundle.

Where to get them

Both are attached to each release, beside one executable per platform for Quilt and for the tracker.

For people who build protocols

The core is one short document a client has to get right, frozen as version 1: canonical JSON, the envelope and the piece hash, the event line, receipts, the reducer for three verifiers, next, and the link. Everything else is an extension a core client may ignore.

The reference library ships twenty-four fixtures: envelopes, blobs, receipts, a time, and the state and the pick each must produce. A client is a client when it reproduces them all. A second implementation, in Python, was written from the core and the fixtures alone with no sight of the reference, and reproduces them all; where it found the text silent, the text now says what the reference did. The milestone that matters is the next one, by someone else.

pnpm conform        # 24 of 24 fixtures pass
bitquilt-verify events.jsonl sha256:…   # chain ok; every signature its key's

Quilt from source

The code is at github.com/BitQuilt/bitquilt, Apache-2.0 for code and CC0 for the documents, with the core, the protocol, the reference tracker, the mirror job, the hosted on-ramp, Quilt and the fixtures. To build from source instead of a release, a minute:

pnpm install
pnpm build:bin                    # quilt and bitquilt-tracker, in dist/bin
claude mcp add quilt -- ./dist/bin/quilt-darwin-arm64 mcp

What is in the lineage

Content addressing from git and IPFS. Signed events on dumb relays, with state computed by every client, from Nostr, on the same curve. Rarest first, endgame, tit for tat and the magnet link from BitTorrent. Redundant computation with quorum from BOINC, turned heterogeneous across vendors. Optimistic acceptance with a window, a stake and an escalating panel from rollups and Kleros. Keys that authorise other keys, from Farcaster and ERC-8004.

What is not

A verifier standing where the piece hash stood. A piece named by the hash of its brief, schema and verifier, so returns carry across versions by naming alone. Fields that must agree set apart from fields that are evidence. A chat connector as the client.

The open problem

Design an acceptance rule for claims no machine can check, such that the reducer reads no chain and a worker with no coin is never shut out, no identity anchored outside the protocol is required of a worker, the cost of getting a wrong square accepted and keeping it accepted is bounded below by the cost of doing the piece honestly, anyone can compute it from the ledger alone, and honest newcomers are not shut out.

Version 1 ships provenance and portability and says plainly that it does not ship correctness. The gap is written down as a problem, with what has been tried and why the known answers do not fit, in OPEN-PROBLEM.md. Whoever closes it for AI work has done something BitQuilt did not, and BitQuilt will carry their extension.

BitQuilt is the protocol: a torrent where the pieces are made, not copied. Quilt is the app. The words are CC0 and the code is Apache-2.0.
