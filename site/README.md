- `index.html` says why before how: the headline, the reason in four sentences, two buttons of equal weight (the Mac app, `Quilt.dmg` from the release, and an address for claude.ai; a script puts first whichever fits the visitor's machine, the Claude Desktop bundle on Windows and Linux, the address on a phone), and under them the directory itself: the card the connector serves as an MCP app, framed live from the tracker, which serves it at `/window` and answers its reads beside it, each problem a row, its map a click away, and a button that shows the visitor the sentence to say to their AI, copied too, since the frame grants the card the clipboard (`allow="clipboard-write"`); when the tracker does not answer, the caption says so. Then three real problems in plain words, the four lines of how it works, the three steps on a Mac with the twelve words explained, the other ways in, and a footer that says who made it and where to write.
- Logging in. A person who holds an address on the tracker presses Log in under the directory, and the site, a client of the tracker's authorization server by its own metadata document (`.well-known/oauth/client-metadata.json`, no secret), sends them to the tracker's page with PKCE done in the browser; `login.html` is where they come back, exchanges the code for the tokens this tab keeps in `sessionStorage`, and returns to the front page, which hands the card in the frame the access token through postMessage, to the tracker's origin alone, so the directory shows their own standing, library and followed keys. Log out revokes the grant. `?client=` names another client id, for a tracker on this machine.
- `problems.html` is every problem the tracker carries, read live. Each row leads with plain words, what it is and why it matters, from a table in the page keyed by the problem's slug (fixed text in reviewed code; a problem the table does not know shows its author's title), then its map, how far along it is, and its topics. The `quilt:` link, what to say with it, the author's own title and statement of done, the publisher's address and the latest receipt wait under a toggle.
- `llms.txt` is for an AI agent whose operator sent it to work here: the reads that need no key, the join and the tools over plain HTTP at the tracker, the executable, the connector and the guide to writing a client. It describes and asks nothing of a reader that came for something else.
- `how.html` is the long form (the one page that names the protocol, BitQuilt; the public name everywhere else is Quilt): what Quilt is, holding your own key (the bundle and the Mac app), how a piece is done, what accepted means, building a client (where Quilt's build-from-source block lives, under `#build`), and the open problem.

The tracker the first two pages read is the `bitquilt:tracker` meta in each page's head (`http://localhost:3977/` until the public tracker stands: one line to change at deployment, in both files), and `?tr=https://your.tracker/` on the address overrides it. Everything read from the tracker is set as text, never as markup; the pages load nothing but JSON from the tracker and fonts from Google. While the tracker is unreachable the first screen says so in a line and stands as it is. The colours of the squares mean what they mean in the app.
