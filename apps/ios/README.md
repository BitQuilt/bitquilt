# Quilt for the iPhone

The iPhone app around a hosted address. It runs no AI: the person's own Claude does the work through the connector at their address ([docs/ext/QX-18.md](../../docs/ext/QX-18.md)). It holds the person's root key and signs what only a root signs. The key the tracker holds for the address is the root's delegate, so the work is the phone's key's and the tracker's key can be ended from the phone ([docs/PROTOCOL.md](../../docs/PROTOCOL.md), Identity: roots and delegates). Beside that, the app is the part a phone is good for: the problems a tracker carries, each with its map, the one step that puts the address into Claude, the sentences to say, each handed to Claude with a tap, and what came back, shown as it happens.

## What it does

- **Words.** The documents name things as the protocol does, and the app says them as a person with a phone would: an answer for a reading, matched for accepted, a score for standing, an identity for the root key, a private link for the address, and Quilt for the tracker (`Sources/Words.swift`). A piece's name is said as words, `claim/5` as Claim 5, a state is said as what happened on the piece and never as the rule it is held to, and the tracker's reason for not counting an answer is said plainly. The protocol's words stay in the documents, and nothing of what is signed or sent changes: a sentence is shown with the problem's title where its link is, and sent with the link.
- **The quilt.** A square is drawn one way everywhere (`Sources/Quilt.swift`): coloured by its piece's state and stitched round its edge, on a problem's map, in a problem's row of the list, in the person's own quilt and in a banner. A square swells for a moment when its state changes, so an answer landing or a match being made is seen where it happens, and breathes while the person's Claude holds its piece. The welcome's quilt is put together square by square, once. A page not yet read shows faint squares in place of a spinner, and an empty page a faint quilt. Where the phone asks for less motion, nothing moves. Page titles are set in the faces the pages use.
- **Problems.** Every problem the tracker carries, searched by every word, those with work under way first, each with its own strip of squares, how many of its pieces are answered and matched, its topics, and how many answers the person's Claude gave there. The strip draws every piece where the problem is small and the same proportions where it is not, and a state with any piece in it keeps a square. A problem opens on its owner's goal, quoted as theirs, and its map, with a legend of every colour the map draws; a square under a finger says what happened on it and links its page.
- **The welcome.** Until Claude is connected the welcome is in front of the tabs (`Sources/WelcomeView.swift`). Its first screen says what Quilt is in one sentence and makes the person's identity and private link with one button, or takes a link they already hold. Its second opens Claude's own Add custom connector window with the link filled in, then asks the tracker every few seconds and moves on by itself once the sheet at the link says an AI has connected, which the tracker records the first time one does. Its last ends on a first piece: it says what the person will meet in Claude, a message to send, a question to allow and an answer to say yes to, and one button opens Claude on a new chat with the sentence that finds a piece; the page then waits with them and says when Claude has taken one. The first answer the phone sees of the person's Claude is said over the whole screen, once, wherever they are in the app: its square sewn in, what Claude said and where the answer stands. A phone that first looks at a link with answers behind it has no first square. Claude's app is on this phone where it answers the scheme `claude`, asked again each time the app comes to the front; where it is not, the page says Claude opens in Safari and links the App Store. A person can look around first, and each button that needs Claude brings the welcome back.
- **You.** The record of the person's Claude, three counts beside each other: the answers it gave, how many another AI matched, and the score, with what the score is and what it is for one tap away. The counts are of the readings the tracker gives of a key, its latest fifty, and the page says so where there are that many. Under them is the person's quilt, a square for each answer, and a button that shares it as a picture (`Sources/Picture.swift`): the squares, how many answers and how many matched, drawn in the light appearance whatever the phone's is, with no name, identity or link on it. Then the things to ask Claude that are not about one problem. Account, behind the gear (`Sources/AccountView.swift`), holds what a person rarely needs and should be able to find: whether Claude is connected, the way the Send button goes, running in the background, where the identity is and how it works, the private link, and the two acts that cannot be undone, each with what it does said beside it.
- **Home.** What to do next, and what the person's Claude did, as the record holds it (`Sources/HomeView.swift`). At the top is the one button that sends Claude to find a piece, and under it what needs a look: a draft that waits for the phone's signature, and what the tracker says is wanted from the person. The app cannot read Claude's chat, and no app can. It does not need to: everything Claude does on Quilt is a signed line on a problem's log, so the app asks the tracker for the work of the key Claude signs with (`/k/<address>/work`) and shows it. Then come what was sent to Claude and not yet picked up, the piece Claude holds now, and each answer with its fields, the passage it rests on, the readings beside it and where it stands: recorded, matched by another AI, or disagreed with, both kept. An answer is whole once it is signed, and the page says so: no answer is shown as waiting. The tracker is asked every three seconds for five minutes after the app comes to the front or a sentence goes to Claude and while Claude holds a piece, every five while an answer is on the record with no square yet, and every twenty otherwise, and only while the app is in front. What changed since the last look is said in a banner over whatever page is in front, with a tap of the phone's haptics, and marked on the Home tab until the page is looked at. A problem's map marks the squares that are the person's Claude's and reads itself again as the work changes. A reading's sheet ends in the sentence that sends Claude back for another piece.
- **Pings.** What changed while the app was not in front is said as a notification on the phone (`Sources/Pings.swift`): an answer of the person's Claude, a match, a disagreement, and a draft that waits for the phone's signature, in the words the banner in the app would have used. No server sends them and no device token leaves the phone. iOS wakes the app now and then, the app reads the work of the key the person's Claude signs with, as it does in front, and posts a notification for what changed since it last looked. iOS decides when, so a ping can come hours after the change, and none comes where Background App Refresh is off; Account says both. Pings are off until the person turns them on, in Account or on the page of their first square, and a look in the background reads nothing where they are off or iOS would not show one, so no change is taken as seen without being said. A tap on a ping opens the answer it is about. A first answer found in the background keeps its page for when the app is next in front.
- **What it keeps.** The app opens on what it last showed (`Sources/Kept.swift`): the person's sheet, their Claude's work, what they sent to Claude, the list of problems and each map looked at are kept on the phone as they change and read back when the app starts, then read again from the tracker at once. With no network the pages still show, under a line that says the tracker could not be reached. Each is written again from what the app read out of the tracker's answer and not as the answer's own bytes, so the sheet kept holds no private link, which stays in the keychain alone. The files are in the caches folder, which the phone may empty and no backup takes, under a name made from the tracker and the link, and signing out removes them.
- **Links.** A square's page on the tracker, `https://tracker.bitquilt.org/r/<problem>/<piece>`, opens the app on that square (`Sources/Links.swift`): the tracker's name is associated with the app in `project.yml`, and the host answers the file Apple reads for it ([docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md), The public door). `quilt_return` names the square's page in what it says, so after an answer the person's Claude can give them the way back. A square of their own Claude's opens on its answer, read again first, and any other on its problem's map with that square chosen. A problem's own link, `quilt:<hash>`, opens the problem's page. A person who has not set up sees what the link names and not the welcome. A link names what to show, and nothing is signed, sent or asked because of one.
- **Asks.** A connector answers Claude's calls and starts none, so this app cannot hand Claude a sentence through it. It leaves the request where Claude reads it: a tap on "Use my existing chat" posts an ask to the address (`/mcp/<token>/asks`), a phrase's name and a problem's hash and no other words, and Claude lists the asks with `quilt_asked` when it next takes a turn (docs/APP.md, What a person asks from their own app). Home shows each ask as ready in the person's chat, where it can be cancelled, or as picked up. The turn comes from the person in the Claude chat they already have, by a press on the ask's button on Quilt's card there or by saying "Do what I asked on Quilt", or from the app itself: in Account, "Run in the background" takes the Fire URL and the token of a scheduled task the person made in Claude, with the prompt the page gives, and from then a tap on "Run in the background" leaves the ask and fires the task, which runs on the person's plan with their connectors, while "Use my existing chat" stays beside it for the chat the person keeps. The URL is taken only where it is a task's fire at Anthropic's API, the token is kept in this phone's keychain and sent there alone, and the fire carries no text (`Sources/Asks.swift`). Not yet tried against Claude's own endpoint from a phone: the request is written as Claude's documentation gives it, and held so by `Tests/AsksTests.swift`.
- **Waiting for you.** A problem is published by a root (CORE.md section 4), and the key the tracker holds for the address is the phone's root's delegate, so a problem the person's Claude drafts is not published. The address keeps the draft and lists it at `/mcp/<token>/waiting` as the text the root signs. Home shows it under "Needs a look", with a banner when it arrives and a mark on the tab while it waits. `Sources/Draft.swift` reads the title, the statement of done and each kind of piece out of that text and nothing else, and signs only where the text is canonical as the phone writes it, the owner is this phone's root, and the hash is the one the draft was listed under; a draft that holds a number that is not whole is refused. "Publish" asks for the person with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode, signs the envelope and the `version` of the problem, and the address posts them. "Not this one" drops the draft and signs nothing.
- **A hand-off.** A Send button sends Claude to work in one tap, by the way the person chose (`Route` in `Sources/Store.swift`): a new chat in Claude with the sentence written in, which is the way until they choose another; the chat they already have, where the ask shows as a button on Quilt's card; or their scheduled task in the background, once they gave the app its trigger. A sentence no ask carries always opens a new chat. The other ways, the choice of which one the button uses, a copy and the share sheet are behind "Other ways to send". A banner that says an ask is ready in the person's chat, and each ask on Home, carries an "Open Claude" button, which opens Claude where the person left it and starts no new chat. Until an AI has connected, a problem's button leads back to the welcome instead, since the sentence would reach a Claude that cannot act on it. The sentence is one of the person's own phrases with a link filled in, never anyone else's words (AGENTS.md rule 6).

- **The key.** `Sources/Root.swift`. At "Make my key and address" the phone makes a secp256k1 key, keeps it in the keychain, joins, and posts a `delegate` from that key to the new address's key before that key has signed anything. An address the person already held is brought under a root that has signed nothing by a `rotate`, which the phone writes and signs as the new root and the on-ramp signs as the old. "End the tracker's key" posts the root's `revoke`. After each, the phone asks the tracker which root the address's key counts for, and the page says where the key is from that answer. A delegation runs ten years, since one cannot be made again to a key that has signed, and the revoke is what ends it.

The root is kept in the keychain, and in iCloud Keychain where the person has that on, so a lost phone does not lose it. The address is its own credential for the tracker's key, so the app keeps it in the keychain too and nowhere else.

## What it signs with

`Sources/Wire.swift` is the wire written for a phone from [docs/v2/WIRE.md](../../docs/v2/WIRE.md) and [docs/CORE.md](../../docs/CORE.md) sections 1 and 4: canonical JSON, Keccak-256, an address with its checksum, a signature over an object's canonical JSON, and an event with its id. The curve comes from one package, `swift-secp256k1`, pinned in `project.yml`, since Apple's own libraries do not carry secp256k1. `Tests/WireTests.swift` holds it to `Tests/vectors.json`, which the core writes:

```bash
node node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs apps/ios/Tests/vectors.ts > apps/ios/Tests/vectors.json
```

`Tests/WireTests.swift` also holds a draft: read from the text it signs, signed as the reference signs it, and refused where it is not what it says. `Tests/KeptTests.swift` holds what the phone keeps of its last read: work read back as it was written, a sheet kept without the private link, and everything removed at a sign-out. `Tests/LinksTests.swift` holds the links that open the app: which are read, and what a link puts in front. `Tests/WorkTests.swift` holds the reading of a key's work, each standing's sentence, what the phone says changed between two looks, the record's counts with the picture's line, and which changes are worth a ping. `Tests/DoorTests.swift` and `Tests/StoreTests.swift` put the root to a real door: a delegation the tracker counts, a rotation, a revoke, the store's join, and a problem drafted through the address and published by the phone's key. They are skipped unless an on-ramp that holds a problem is named, and the store's run in the simulator alone, since they write the keychain:

```bash
TEST_RUNNER_QUILT_TEST_TRACKER=http://localhost:3977/ xcodebuild test -project Quilt.xcodeproj -scheme Quilt -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 16 Pro'
```

## What it reads

All of it is the tracker's own HTTP, which [docs/PROTOCOL.md](../../docs/PROTOCOL.md) lists: `POST /join`, the sheet as JSON beside an address, `/search`, a problem's `/state`, one problem's `/scrape` where a link names it, a key's `/k/<address>/standing` and `/k/<address>/work`, the identity log at `/k/events`, and the address's `/rotate` and `/waiting`. The tracker is `https://tracker.bitquilt.org/`; a run in the simulator names another with the launch argument `-tracker`, an address with `-address`, the tab in front with `-tab you`, whether Claude's app is there with `-claude yes` or `-claude no`, the newest reading's sheet opened with `-reading first` or the newest draft's with `-reading draft`, a link followed at launch with `-open`, one look made as a look in the background makes it with `-ping now`, and the question for the person left out with `-presence no`.

## Building

XcodeGen writes the Xcode project and the Info.plist from `project.yml`, and neither is committed.

```bash
cd apps/ios && xcodegen generate
```

```bash
./build-testflight.sh 2 --upload
```

The script archives and exports build 2 and, with `--upload`, sends it to TestFlight; its header says which names it reads from the environment. The faces are the Mac app's, read from `apps/mac/Resources/Fonts`.

## What is not built yet

- A push the moment something changes. It needs a server that holds device tokens, so the app's pings come when iOS next wakes it, which can be hours later.
- The coin. The app shows standing alone.
- Any way to work a piece without the person's own Claude.
- A draft's rows shown before it is signed. The phone signs the hash of each data blob, which the envelope names, and shows the briefs with their slots unfilled.
- A stake rule or a gold posted from the phone, and the owner's hourly turn for a problem the phone's root owns: sharpening a brief and taking a proposed piece each ask the root's signature, and nothing asks the phone for it yet.
- The root shown as twelve words, or carried to a Quilt on another machine.
