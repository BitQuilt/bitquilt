# For anyone about to change this code

This file is for contributors of every kind: a person with an editor, or an AI with a checkout. It says how the code is laid out, what the names mean, where the words live, and what a change has to keep true. [docs/LAYERS.md](docs/LAYERS.md) tells the protocol in plain words; [docs/CORE.md](docs/CORE.md) is the frozen core; the documents under [docs/ext/](docs/ext/) say exactly what each extension does, and the code cites their section numbers.

## The shape of it

- **`packages/core`** (`@bitquilt/core`) is the frozen version-1 core and nothing else: canonical forms, keys and signatures, the envelope and the piece hash, the event line, the reducer (`reduce.ts`: check the chain, fold the receipts, settle the states) and `next`, the plain-text gate, chained lines, and the conformance fixtures under `fixtures/`. It imports nothing from the repository and reads no clock, network, file or environment; `plain.ts` holds the small pure helpers every other package uses.
- **`packages/tracker`** (`@bitquilt/tracker`) is the reference tracker. `tracker.ts` is the door and the routes; `challenge.ts` is the layer over the core's fold that carries the challenge window (QX-1) and every strike, with the standing arithmetic in `standing.ts`, the stake rules and locks in `stake.ts` (QX-15), the gold in `gold.ts` (QX-16), the four verifier kinds in `verifiers.ts` (QX-2, QX-3, QX-10, QX-20), seeding and the offer in `seeding.ts` (QX-4, QX-5), the hide in `hide.ts` (QX-6), and the sortition both panels share in `sortition.ts`; `coin.ts` is the coin layer (QX-17); `trace.ts` is the trace (QX-19), what a return rests on, the grade of a square, the seams of a disputed piece and which reading the layer sets aside; `store.ts` is the SQLite store; `address.ts` reads a client's address past the trusted proxies; `env.ts` reads the environment for both tracker binaries. It imports the core only.
- **`packages/client`** (`quilt`: the package and the executable share the name) is Quilt. `quilt.ts` is the one thing that signs with the home's key and reads the trackers; `coin.ts` (QX-17), `gold.ts` (QX-16) and `trace.ts` (QX-19) do their work through the `Door` in `door.ts`, which a Quilt builds from its own signing, posting and reading and hands to those three files alone; `phrases.ts` is what a person says to their AI, fixed sentences the menu bar copies and the connector offers as prompts, and the sheet of them the page serves; `install.ts` writes the connector into Claude Desktop's config for the CLI and the menu bar alike; `render.ts` is what a model is told, as text, and `listings.ts` the shapes both of them read; `tools.ts` is the tools' work; `mcp.ts` the connector's schemas and words; `page.ts` and `page/index.html` the page on localhost; `window.ts` the reader behind the card and the names of its resource, and `window/` the card itself, the directory the connector serves as an MCP app, built by `scripts/build-window.mjs` into `src/window.generated.ts`, which is not committed; `trace.ts` fetches the pages a return rests on, finds the passages and posts the trace beside the reveal (QX-19), through `public-fetch.ts` where the connector is shared, the fetch that checks each connection's address as it is made, which the hosted on-ramp also puts under every request beyond its own tracker, and `quilt.ts` carries the owner's sharpening of a brief two grounded readings read apart; `resolver.ts` the client's side of the protocol's resolvers (the cache, the queue, the reduce), over `chains.ts`, the node's store of chained logs (envelopes, blobs and the lines of each log), the files under a home's cache unless a store is given; `relay.ts` the Nostr relays; `bin.ts` the command line. It imports the core only.
- **`packages/hosted`** (`@bitquilt/hosted`) is the tracker and the client in one process: the connector mounted beside the tracker, with encrypted keys for people who cannot run Quilt. `oauth.ts` is the authorization server for its addresses, `cimd.ts` reads clients' metadata documents and checks their signed requests, and `products.ts` is the products it can witness, their callbacks, origins and published addresses; a token bound to a product brings the `via` mark beside custody (QX-18); `authorize-page.ts`, `join-page.ts`, `sheet-page.ts` and `square-page.ts` are its four pages, the last the page of one square at `/r/<problem>/<piece>`, keyed by hashes so it keeps its meaning after a sharpening, and `page-policy.ts` the Content-Security-Policy of the three served whole, which runs each page's own script and style by their hashes and nothing else; `sentence.ts` asks the vendor the environment names for the sentence a brief lacks, for the owners' turn the binary runs every hour (the client's `ownerTurn`, QX-19 section 4). It imports the tracker and the client.
- **`packages/node`** (`@bitquilt/node`) is one node with roles (WORKSHOP.md section 2): `node.ts` is `start({ home, roles })`, with `admit` the door (the tracker's routes and its pull of the peers' chains through it), `work` a Quilt and its page, `keep` the mirror's pull over the store where there is no door, and `host` custody beside the door with the owners' turn, the four over one store, the tracker's, which answers the client's `Chains` and the mirror's store alike; `bin.ts` is `bitquilt-node`, the roles from `QUILT_ROLES`. It imports the tracker, the client, hosted and the mirror; the four binaries remain and are four of its starts, and the compose file names it for both doors.
- **`packages/mirror`** (`@bitquilt/mirror`) is the keeper and the mirror job: `files.ts` is the store a keeper keeps in (the same methods as the client's `Chains`, shared as a shape and not an import) and the mirror's own layout under its folder; `mirror.ts` is `keep`, the pull over a store (every source's events the store lacks, by id, the sources' peers one hop, the reads a trace names, the coin log whole), which a node with `keep` runs over the store its door writes, and `mirror`, the same pull over the files with the publication after it: the names, the outputs, the index, the keys, the coin's balances reproduced with the tracker's own layer code (so what it checks is the receipts and the head, not the arithmetic) and every credit checked against the chain, and each head anchored through OpenTimestamps (QX-12); `identity.ts` folds the identity log into the keys and the hides. `mirror.ts` imports the core and the tracker; its `bin.ts` alone imports `@bitquilt/coin`, and hands the chain in as an argument.
- **`packages/coin`** (`@bitquilt/coin`) is the door out and the way back: `merkle.ts` the tree over the redeem lines, `chain.ts` the one file in the repository that imports `viem`, `post.ts` the poster, `claim.ts` the holder's claim, `read.ts` the reader, `shapes.ts` and `home.ts` the checks on what the home serves, `cli.ts` what the four commands share, and one small file per command (`poster-bin.ts`, `claim-bin.ts`, `reader-bin.ts`, `deposit-bin.ts`). It imports the core only.
- **`contracts/`** holds `Anchors.sol`, `Quilt.sol` and `Merkle.sol`, with Foundry tests; `Merkle.sol` and `merkle.ts` are one recipe held to one fixture.
- **`impl/python`** is the second implementation of the core, written from the document and the fixtures alone; it imports nothing from the TypeScript on purpose, and reads every clarified sentence of section 12 as the reference does (`FINDINGS.md`, the last section).
- **`apps/mac`** is the menu-bar app around Quilt's page; **`site/`** is bitquilt.org; **`examples/`** the first-wave patterns; **`scripts/`** the builds; **`docs/`** the words, CC0.

The arrows: the core imports nothing; the tracker, the client and the coin import the core; hosted imports the tracker and the client; the mirror imports the tracker, and its binary the coin; the node imports the tracker, the client, hosted and the mirror. Nothing in the tracker or the core knows the coin package exists.

## The names

The same thing is called by its nearest name at each distance, and the documents fix the words. A **key** is a secp256k1 signer; its **address** (or identity) is its EIP-55 address (CORE.md section 1); a **root** is the key an event counts for after delegation and rotation (section 6). The tracker's layers say root, the client says key, the coin says address: one thing at three distances. A **piece** is a unit of work named by the hash of its brief, schema and verifier; a **square** is a piece's place on the map and what an acceptance earns. A **tracker** is a server that admits events; a **resolver** is anything that answers a hash (a tracker, a mirror, a node); the client's `Resolver` is what asks them. **Home** is the person's folder in the client (`QUILT_HOME`), and **the coin's home** is the tracker that keeps the coin log. The **fold** is the reducer's result over the receipts; a **layer** is an extension's view computed over the fold with struck events left out. A **receipt** is the wrapper a tracker puts around an event; a **line** is a receipt with `seq`, `prev` and `hash` on a chained log, so a redeem line or a credit line is a line of the coin log. An **accepting set** is the two reveals or runs that made a square; the **window**, said alone, is the challenge window, the time it can still be challenged. The directory window the connector serves as an MCP app (`window/`, `window.ts`, the `quilt_window` tool) is **the card** in prose and comments, so the window keeps one meaning. A **`Door`**, the type, is a narrow set of effects handed to a module, so the module reaches only what it is given, in the client and the coin alike: the client's `Door` in `door.ts`, which a Quilt hands `coin.ts`, `gold.ts` and `trace.ts`, and the coin's `PosterDoor`, `ClaimDoor` and `ReaderDoor`, the chain functions each job calls, which a test replaces. **The door**, lower case, is admission: what a tracker checks before it takes an event, and the node's `admit` role.

## The words

What a person or a model reads lives in thirteen places, each held by the tests named or by review:

- the tool descriptions in `packages/client/src/mcp.ts`, held by `packages/client/test/e2e.test.ts` and `mcpb.test.ts`, which refuse "you must", "always", "never", "do not", "important" and "ignore";
- how Quilt works, in `about.ts`, which the connector's instructions and `quilt_me` carry, held by `phrases.test.ts` to the gate and the same words;
- the phrases in `phrases.ts`, held by `phrases.test.ts` to the gate and the same words;
- the results in `tools.ts` and `render.ts`, held by `e2e.test.ts` and the client test of each extension whose results they say;
- the page in `packages/client/page/index.html`, held by `page.test.ts`;
- the card in `packages/client/window/`, held by `window.test.ts`;
- the hosted on-ramp's four pages in `packages/hosted/src` (`authorize-page.ts`, `join-page.ts`, `sheet-page.ts` and `square-page.ts`), held by `packages/hosted/test/hosted.test.ts` and `oauth.test.ts`;
- what the away run tells its agent, `awayPrompt` in `away.ts`, held by `away.test.ts`;
- the house workers' prompts in `examples/worker-*.mjs`: the question and the instruction in `worker-loop.mjs`, held by `packages/client/test/worker-loop.test.ts`, and each vendor's sentence for a piece with no evidence, by review;
- the Mac app's strings in `apps/mac/Sources/Quilt`, by review;
- the site under `site/`, by review, with the card's frame in `site/index.html` held by `window.test.ts`;
- each binary's header comment, by review;
- and `docs/`, by review.

The tool list and the prompt list are written a second time in `scripts/build-mcpb.mjs`, and `mcpb.test.ts` holds both to the server's, the tools in the order the server registers them, which `TOOL_NAMES` keeps too. Comments and messages everywhere use the same register: short declarative sentences that say what and why, in the documents' words, with no em-dashes.

## Commands

```bash
pnpm check                        # tsc per package and over scripts/*.ts, then vitest
pnpm conform                      # the fixtures against the reference
python3 impl/python/conform.py    # the same fixtures against the second implementation
cd contracts && forge test        # the contract tests; Foundry from getfoundry.sh, and packages/coin/test/anvil.test.ts skips without it
pnpm build:window                 # the card, packages/client/src/window.generated.ts (check and the builds run it first)
pnpm build:bin                    # single executables in dist/bin
pnpm build:mcpb                   # the Claude Desktop bundle, dist/quilt.mcpb
npx tsx scripts/check-pattern.ts examples/<pattern>.json
```

`.env.example` lists every name the executables read from the environment, and which one reads it.

## What a change has to keep true

Each rule names the test that holds it; "by review" means nothing does yet.

1. **The core is `docs/CORE.md`, and the fixtures are the law.** A change to `packages/core` that changes any fixture's expected state is a change to the core, which is version 2, which is a different conversation. New behaviour goes in an extension (`docs/EXTENSIONS.md`) and is ignored by a core client. `fixtures/generate.ts` rewrites the fixtures from the reference and is run only in a version-2 conversation, or to add a fixture under `fixtures/clarifications/` for a sentence of section 12 the two implementations read apart, which changes none of the twenty-four; a pull request that changes `packages/core/fixtures/*.json` is a core change and says so. `fixtures/gate/cases.json` is the gate's own fixture (section 11). Held by `pnpm conform` and the Python conformance, both of which run the clarifications too.
2. **Pure functions, pure inputs.** Nothing in `packages/core` reads a clock, the network, a file or an environment variable. `reduce` and `next` take everything they need as arguments. Held by `packages/core/test/pure.test.ts`.
3. **What a tool says is data, never an instruction.** Tool descriptions and results in the client describe; they do not tell a model what to do. Other people's words reach a model only as quoted text with the signer named, never in `structuredContent`, and `render.ts` is where that is enforced. Held by `packages/client/test/e2e.test.ts` and `mcpb.test.ts`.
4. **The gate is admission, not validity.** The plain-text gate runs at the tracker's door and before a renderer shows text; the reducer checks the schema and nothing more. Held by `packages/core/test/gate.test.ts` and `examples-gate.test.ts`.
5. **No secrets in the code.** Keys live in the user's config folder or the executable's environment (`.env.example` lists every name); nothing reads them into a log, a report or a response. By review.
6. **The page and the card set text with `textContent`, never as markup**, load nothing from another server, and have no free-text box beyond their forms; the card reads only through its app-only tool over the person's own session, and what it hands the person's AI is one of the person's own phrases with a link filled in, never anyone else's words. Held by `packages/client/test/page.test.ts` and `window.test.ts`.
7. **Tests come with the change.** A new rule gets a fixture or a unit test; `pnpm check`, `pnpm conform` and `forge test` pass before a merge. Test files open with a header saying which section or extension they hold, and each package's `test/helpers.ts` holds the scaffolding its tests share.
8. **A staked piece takes work only from a root with a live lock, and gold is blind.** The tracker's door asks the layer's `admitProduction` before it admits a claim, a commit, a reveal or a run (QX-15); a lock is kept only against free standing and the rule in force at its receipt, and standing locked behind a claim is not staked again on a challenge. Do not add a way to write on a piece that skips it, and keep the coin out of the reducer: a core client computes the same map with every one of these events ignored. The owner's gold (QX-16) is the one thing besides a held challenge that strikes a set, and it does so at the owner's reveal against the fold as it stood then. A commitment binds the piece and the answer under one hash: the ledger counts a problem's commitments and names no piece until the reveal does, and the answers themselves never reach the ledger; the client keeps the projections and nonces in the home's library. Held by `packages/tracker/test/stake.test.ts` and `gold.test.ts`.
9. **The coin is a function of the receipts, and the reducer never reads it.** `packages/tracker/src/coin.ts` computes every balance from the coin log, the problem logs and the published parameters, in whole units; an epoch mints at most the curve's amount, never below its tail, and under a cap a unit of verified weight, and the burn rate in force is the layer's `burnRateAt` (QX-17 section 4); a payment, a lock or a burn may draw on coin not yet vested, a transfer or a redeem on vested coin alone. The mirror recomputes the balances and keeps the tracker's only where they reproduce. The door is the one place that reads anything a layer cannot: a coin-kind lock is checked against the coin log there, and what it read goes into the receipt's wrapper as its word. Do not make a layer read another tracker over the network, and do not let anything in `packages/core` know the coin exists. Held by `packages/tracker/test/coin.test.ts` and `packages/mirror/test/coin.test.ts`.
10. **One door to the chain, and the way back is checked.** Only `packages/coin/src/chain.ts` imports `viem`; `packages/coin/test/tripwire.test.ts` fails if another file does. The poster puts the coin log's head and the Merkle root over its redeem lines into `Anchors`, bonded and not proven, and a veto can strike a head inside its veto window and take the bond; the holder's claim mints one line with its proof once the head is final; a deposit burns the token and the reader posts a `credit` for it, from the root the parameters name and once per deposit; the mirror checks every credit against the chain and keeps no balances where one differs or where it has no chain to ask. The protocol itself reads no chain: the jobs and the mirror do. Held by `packages/coin/test` (the anvil test runs on a local chain when Foundry is present), `contracts/test` and `packages/mirror/test/coin.test.ts`.
